# Frost & Thaw - creature design guide

Distilled from Clip Studio's monster-design guide, Kyle Brown's Introduction to Creature
Design (Gnomon), Creativepool's creature tips, notes on Pokemon design, pixel-art
selective-outline tutorials, and the user's own references (Cofagrigus, Runerigus,
Sinistea / Polteageist, Sinistcha / Poltchageist).

## 1. Concept before drawing
- Answer: what does it do, how does it move, how does it express itself, what is its
  story role? (A possessed instrument *performs*; Alberica's line is a cheerful bard who
  makes a dark pact - happy, mischievous, never grim.)
- Pick the category (animal / humanoid / object) and the one or two real things it is
  built from. Ground every part in a real reference (a real lute's bent pegbox, a real
  grand piano's bentside curve) so it reads instantly.

## 2. Process (do not skip steps)
1. **Thumbnails**: many quick two-value silhouettes. Go past the obvious first idea.
   Throw away weak ones early. Show the user a spread of distinct options, not tweaks.
2. **Pick and refine** a silhouette: loose line + light value; check it at small size.
3. **Head / feature variations** on the chosen body; swap parts between sketches.
4. **Colour and render** only after the form is approved.
5. **3/4 pose** (front sprite) and **back view** (player's side) - the back must be
   designed, not a recolour.
6. **Checks**: two-value silhouette test; grayscale test (colours stay distinct);
   readable at 96px and at icon size; recognisable from its best angle; remembered
   after looking away.

## 3. Shape and silhouette
- 2-6 primitive shapes (spheres, cones, ellipsoids, deformed and joined); 2-6 colours.
- Shape language: round = friendly, angular = dangerous; "happy dark" mixes round bodies
  with a few sharp accents (a fang, horns, a jagged crack).
- Asymmetry makes a design distinctive (one big hand, a tilted pose, a chip on one side).
- Strong, simple outer silhouette; detail lives inside it.

## 4. Object Pokemon (Cofagrigus, Runerigus, Sinistea, Polteageist, Chandelure)
- The object stays clean, elegant and recognisable - it is not turned into a cartoon.
- The face is designed into the object (carved mask, painted swirl ornament, a rune).
- The possessing spirit is a separate substance (goo, smoke, shadow) seen through
  chips and cracks, peeking out with small limbs, or reaching out as big bold
  appendages that fill the frame.
- Breaking the object is allowed and useful: it shows what's inside.
- Limited palette: object colours + one spirit colour.

## 5. Evolution lines
- Shared motif and consistent colours through the line (here: gold trim, strings,
  violet spirit, swirl ornament).
- Later stages are larger and more complex; head proportion shrinks.
- A dramatic change is fine if one idea unifies it (lute -> cello -> grand piano:
  bigger instruments of the same bard).
- Types are signalled by colour and form (Dark: shadow, sly grin; Fairy: pink glow,
  sparkles, ribbons).

## 6. Pixel art rules (96x96 battle sprites)
- Draw smooth shapes and reduce them; never let curves become stair-step noise.
- 1px outlines, no doubled corners; selective outline = the colour beside the edge,
  one shade darker (lighter on the lit side).
- 3-4 tones per material in flat, clean clusters; no single-pixel noise, no dither
  speckle, no pillow shading (light from one direction, upper left).
- Thin parts (strings, pegs) need deliberate handling: draw them as clean 1px lines or
  solid knobs, never as accidental speckles.
- Keep the figure large in the frame; leave breathing room for appendages.
