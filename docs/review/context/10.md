# Context pass 10 · Chapter 10: Trials of the Roots

What a newcomer can and can't follow in the trial week, judged only from what plays in 0.7.3 (`traces_073/journey.txt` 8638–10033, the three Herald traces at the same flags), with `script/10.md` for staging where the run and the script agree. It builds on `notes/10.md`, `story/10.md` and `dialogue/10.md` and doesn't repeat their staging and line fixes; it assumes notes E1 (Heralds on screen: 0.7.3 now stages most Herald lines with `Stage.herald`, but every named NPC who "comes with you" is still absent), E2 (a clock), E4 (hub goal: now built as "Trial week, day n of 7: k left. Next: ...") and E12 (Phildendrin's entrance).

The week's words are mostly good. What's missing is the frame a newcomer needs to read a court: who sits in judgement and why they're entitled to, what the player is to the court, what the crystals do, what each charge actually accuses someone of having *done*, and what is at stake in the vote. Two charges try things the player never saw (Alberica's spores, Reptin's "god"), one judge confessed to a worse crime two chapters ago (Selrae), and the one Herald whose own mode shows the night in question (Wyn) gives an account that contradicts it. The fix that carries most of it is the one Nicklin names: use the court scene as a **format** (p. 92). Everyone knows what a court looks like: a bench, a dock, a charge read aloud, a witness called, a verdict. Where the week borrows that shape, a newcomer needs no explaining; where it doesn't (a hearing held in a corridor, a verdict as a list), nothing tells them what they're watching.

**Owner's calls this part depends on** (marked ⚖ in the tables): what happens to Selrae, and what Alberica is really charged with. Options are in §3, M10-2 and M10-3. Every other fix works with either answer.

They don't know what they are to it, what "the crystals hear" means, what a witness does, or that anyone is forbidden to leave. Meat Meat is charged with murder and then wanders the city all week. ### M10-2 · Selrae on the bench: (b), an owner's call ⚖
