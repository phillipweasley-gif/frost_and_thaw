# Context pass 11 · Chapter 11: The Frost Saint Walks

What a newcomer can and can't follow in Chapter 11, judged only from what plays in 0.7.3 (`traces_073/journey.txt` 10034–10271, from *END OF ACT II* to `ch11_done`; the Alberica, Wyn and Reptin traces at the same flags). It builds on `notes/11.md`, `story/11.md` and `dialogue/11.md`, and on part 10's fixes (10.md M10-7 names Solstice and the Skytower griffons before this chapter; M10-8 plants the relit candles round Sephek's ice). 0.7.3 has already built: Iskra as a roc who flies in on screen; the Heralds staged at the Hearth, the Shrine and the Council; the White Vault held back for the postgame (the sigils hum instead of merging); the relics kept in the Goal menu.

The chapter is short and its big beats are clear: Auril speaks to Alberica, the Halo raise Sephek, Iskra takes Frostina, the Council sends the party north. What a newcomer lacks is the frame around them. Nobody tells the player why *they* can see Auril, who the people on the altar are (both were last seen four chapters ago), how the Halo got the heart, or how Frostina's capture touches the player's own goal, the Wall. The Act III adhesive is the gap that matters most: the player is home, and nothing on screen says why they must leave it.

**The adhesive** (Bell pp. 87–89; WRITING_GUIDE §3b: "say it at the start of each act and again when it changes"). This is the start of Act III, and the adhesive changes.

- *Why the Heralds go:* Frostina (Alerion: "She's mine to watch"), and Alberica ("If she wants me, she can ask me to my face"). **Said**, in 11.09.
- *Why the player goes:* **missing.** They're in their own city, in their own time, with Hellen's Hearth a lift-ride away. The player's goal has been "Put the wheel right, and the Wall between their time and yours comes down" since Chapter 6. The game's own rule (Lumin, Ch6: "The wyrms can't turn the wheel of the seasons alone") means that with Frostina gone the wheel can't turn and the Wall never comes down. Nobody says so. This is the walk-away test at the act's doorway (Bell pp. 33–37): as played, the player *could* stay home, and nothing would be lost that they've been told about. Fix: M11-4 boxes 12–14 (Lumin says it to the player, the one person here whose goal it is), M11-5's choice (the player says why they go), and the Goal menu's "Your own" line updated.

### M11-3 · Iskra: does it land? (b) small changes inside 11.06

**As built in 0.7.3** it nearly does. The setup is on the golden path: at the Requiem (Ch3), Luna Carver walks over and says "The night the Rime began, the Frostmaiden's roc flew over us. / Iskra. A white bird, wide as a sail. Something fell from her. / An egg, cold all through." That gives the newcomer her name, what she is, how big, whose, and what she dropped. In Chapter 11 she is on screen: a white bird drops four tiles out of the top of the screen, "a vast white roc drops out of the night", "Iskra.", and Alerion: "That's the roc. The bird that dropped her egg." She leaves north with the crystal and the riders (both fade as she rises). A newcomer can say what happened, what next (north) and roughly why.

**Where it slips:**

1. **One setup, seven chapters back.** Luna's line plays around box 1,400 of the log; Iskra flies in around box 10,150. A player who played across many sessions won't hold it (Nicklin pp. 95–101). The motif rule (WRITING_GUIDE §3a) wants three on the path. One plant in between is enough: in 10.07's Hearth night (on the path: resting is how the days pass), *That night, up at the crown, Frostina sings at the north for an hour. Nobody knows why.* *(suggestion)*
2. **Whose bird.** Chapter 11 never says she's Auril's. Alerion's "the bird that dropped her egg" ties her to Frostina, not to the Frostmaiden. Then Sephek's "I'm taking her home" points at a home the player hasn't seen, and Alerion's "She's not hers" has two possible "her"s (Iskra's? Auril's?).
3. **Scale and the sprite.** "Vast", "wide as a sail", but the sprite is a follower-sized flying Articuno. A Pokémon player will read it as Articuno, a different, famous bird; a newcomer sees a bird no bigger than the Heralds. The bible's art list already wants Iskra BIG (P2, not started). Until then a shadow crossing the Canopy before she appears sells the size for free.
4. **Sound.** The Canopy's Azalea Town plays through the whole capture; no cry (notes E7).
5. **The bible's beat 2** ("Alerion, her Dragoon, is thrown down trying to stop them") isn't shown: Alerion appears already lying in the snow.
