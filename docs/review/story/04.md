# Story review 04 · Chapter 4: Dougan's Hole

Judged against the as-played script (`docs/review/script/04.md`, scenes 4.01–4.25 plus side content), the design (`story_bible.md`, `act1_script.md` SC 4.01–4.10) and the earlier chapters for setups. Line, staging and bug findings are in `docs/review/notes/04.md` and are referred to, not repeated.

---

## 1. The chapter in one line

**As the player would say it from the script alone:** *"Who has been calling us?" Granny Thredra, a hag who serves Auril. She sent the relics so the Frostmaiden could find "a new coat to wear", and she has frozen the town's people in her mirrors. But first: can we bring Silja and Finn home? Yes.*

**Does the game say the question?** Yes, twice, which is rare in Act I. Chapter 3 ends on the goal "Bryn Shander's east gate, then south-east to Dougan's Hole. Find who's calling us." (3.19), and the night sets "The Twenty Stones. Find who's calling." (4.21). The answer is a line, not an inference: "THREDRA: I sent them. My watchers watched you aboard." (4.22).

**Does it answer the question the last chapter raised?** Yes, exactly, and it hands on cleanly: "Come and find me in the chasm, dearies. Bring your wolf." and Durmoot's "make sure she never comes back up out of it" (4.24–4.25) set Chapter 5's "Can we stop her?".

**But the question is parked for most of the chapter.** From 4.05 to 4.20 (15 of 25 scenes) the chapter runs on a different, locally stated question, Durmoot's "Wolves took Hilda's kids. Silja and Finn. They're dead.", and nobody mentions the calling. The hag only appears because the gym badge arms her night (switch 3921). So as played the chapter is two stories end to end: a rescue, then a reveal.

**Gameplay vs text.** This chapter's spine is mostly carried by play: following the wolves, the lodge (corridor, kobolds, cage puzzle), the ambush, the gym, three mirror fights and the hag. Text carries the catalyst, the midpoint (Norsu), the bite and the reveal.

**Milestones.** No game-wide Berger stake falls here, but the **bible's whole-game throughline gets its first answer**: the rewrite "adds one throughline: who called the Heralds north, and why". "Who" is answered here (Thredra). "Why" is half-answered ("She wants a new coat to wear, you see. I'm only her seamstress.") and then contradicted by her own offer to end the winter, which is the seed for Chapter 5's "she wants more". It lands as a speech, without the rule behind it (problem 3). The chapter also holds Berger's **exposure of weakness** for Reptin (p. 83): his flaw, giving too much, becomes a wound that will cost him later.

## 7. Game fit

- **Story as reward.** Mostly. The homecoming rewards the rescue, the thaw rewards the mirrors, and the hag's reveal comes after the badge, so it reads as the next level opening. The parley is the one wall: 18 boxes before a choice.
- **Scene/sequel (Mills pp. 108–109).** The ambush (scene) → homecoming (sequel) works. The gym → night does not: the gym has no disaster, and the night is triggered by the badge, not by anything the story did. Momentum is cause and effect (Seger pp. 79–80), and here the cause is a switch.
- **Conflict vs challenge.** The best match in Act I: caged children → a cage puzzle; liars → a choice that rewards catching the lie; a hag who keeps people in mirrors → you fight what climbs out of them.
- **Choices.** Real and readable in play (§4). The climax's bargain is the exception.

## 8. Verdict

**What's working**

1. **The rescue is played, not told.** The cage alone reads four earlier actions. This is how the rest of Act I should feel.
2. **The image.** "Those aren't fishers. Fishers fidget." is the best place introduction in the game.
3. **Reptin's arc gets its root** in an action the player helps choose, and the hag uses it at once.
4. **Durmoot's small, complete arc**, from "Either you're lost or you're stupid" to "...Don't tell anyone I said that."

**Top 3 story problems**

1. **The question waits behind an unconnected subplot.** *Why it hurts:* 15 scenes of rescue never touch the calling, and the hag arrives because of a badge, so the chapter's answer feels bolted on. *Fix:* braid the strands. Let the wolves already be hers: at the ambush, *(suggestion)* KANAN: "Granny pays better than the giant ever did." Give Silja one line about the laughing *(suggestion)*: "Something laughed under the ice every night. It knew our names." Trigger the night from the homecoming rest (the design's own trigger, "try to rest"), not from the badge. Seger: a subplot must intersect and push the plot (pp. 63–64, 75), and each scene should hold the seed of the next (pp. 79–80).
2. **Shimmer's death leaves no trace on the spine.** *Why it hurts:* one chapter after the first ally death, her sibling is quipping ("Thirteen miles of this. Wonderful."), and the egg holding her heart isn't mentioned once. The grief is all optional. *Fix:* give Shade a line when Thredra names the door (notes E17); move the smokehouse banter ("Shimmer liked smoked trout. Hated the smell. Ate it anyway.") onto the homecoming rest; *(suggestion)* let the Frost Egg go cold in Shade's coat when the laughter starts. Berger: a major beat needs consequences later (pp. 112–113); Seger: an arc needs beats across the acts (p. 255–256).
3. **The throughline's answer lands as a speech without its rules.** *Why it hurts:* "I sent them" is the game's first big reveal, but its "watchers" were never seen, the rule that makes the mirrors matter is never said, and her offer to end the winter contradicts "I'm only her seamstress" without anyone noticing. *Fix:* *(suggestion)* plant one watcher earlier, a reflection that lags half a beat (world rule 1 already names it) in Targos or Lonelywood; add Alerion's "She keeps them in the glass. Break it, they go free." (notes E15); let someone voice the contradiction as Chapter 5's hook *(suggestion)*: REPTIN: "A seamstress who wants to end her mistress's winter. Curious." Seger: a twist must be foreshadowed and never feel like a cheat (pp. 86–88); Mills: plant foreshadowing for every surprise (p. 123).

**Rewrite sketch: the night of the hag (4.19–4.24).** New material is marked *(suggestion)*.

1. **Homecoming, staged** (notes E3–E4): Silja and Finn run to Hilda's door on screen; Hilda, Durmoot at her door. Cut the brother joke.
2. **Rest at the Knucklehead Smokehouse** (or Hilda's). The owner's smoked-trout banter plays here: "Shimmer liked smoked trout..." This is now the night's quiet beat.
3. **The laughter.** The night tint comes as you wake, not at the gym door. Relics burn. *(suggestion)* Narration: *In Shade's coat, the Frost Egg goes cold.* SHADE: "She doesn't like it either."
4. **Out to the Stones.** "Durmoot told us to stay in." / "Durmoot tells everyone to stay in." / Meat Meat is already outside.
5. **The roll call.** Thredra names the relics; one narration beat per Herald (Reptin's hand on his medallion, Shade turning the dagger). At "She already carried a door.": *(suggestion)* SHADE: "Say her name." / THREDRA: "Names are for the living, dearie."
6. **The reveal and the bargain.** "I sent them." / "I'm only her seamstress." / "this one isn't anywhere at all". The offer to Reptin and the choice. Alerion: "She keeps them in the glass. Break it, they go free."
7. **Three mirrors, three truths** *(suggestion, replaces three identical scenes)*: before each fight, the glass shows one Herald something true. Shade sees Shimmer turning the carved cat. Reptin sees himself with a wolf's eyes. Alberica sees herself old (seeding the Chapter 5 ghost). Each costs a line; each mirror still breaks into "a hundred laughing pieces".
8. **Thredra's battle**, then her flight shown (shards blowing south) and a pan to the lake as the shapes fall to their knees.
9. **The husband**, on screen; then one thawed voice: "She sat on the ice and drank tea with us... I told her everything."
10. **Morning.** Durmoot comes down the hill with the lantern. "Thank you. ...Don't tell anyone I said that."

What changes: the night follows the rescue instead of the badge, Shimmer is remembered where it matters, and the mirrors show the rule they embody (every mirror shows something true and hides something else) instead of repeating one scene three times.
