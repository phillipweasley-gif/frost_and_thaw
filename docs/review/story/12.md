# Part 12 · Chapter 12: The Sea of Moving Ice — story review

Sources: as-played script `docs/review/script/12.md` (its scene numbers), design `docs/story/story_bible.md` (Ch 12,
Postgame, the five rules) and `docs/story/act3_script.md` (SC 12.01–12.06), notes `docs/review/notes/12.md` (cited
as "notes E1" etc.). Part 12 as played also contains Ch 11's optional relics (11.11) and the White Vault, which the
bible puts in the postgame; this review takes the part as the player gets it.

## 1. The chapter in one line

**Question (as a player would put it):** "Is Avarice really dead, and can we get to Auril's island?"
**Answer:** no, she lived in the mirrors; you break her body in the White Vault and her last splinter flies ahead
to Auril's throne. Then you fly north and land, with nothing in the way.

**Does the game say the question?** The bible's question for Ch 12 is "Can we reach her?". The game **never** says it
or anything like it. The nearest lines are Tamsin's "You're going to fight a GOD. With THAT team?" (12.13) and
Sopo's "She's expecting you!" (12.16), and neither is a question about *reaching* her. The question the chapter
actually dramatises is Alberica's: "You're dead. We watched you die." (12.08).

**Does it answer the question the last chapter raised?** No. Ch 11 ended on two questions: "Can we get Frostina
back before Auril pours herself into three hearts?" and Reptin's "we decide what happens to Auril" (11.07). Ch 12
touches neither. The question it answers, "What's left of Avarice?", is word for word the bible's **postgame**
question (the chapter table: "PG · The White Vault · What's left of Avarice?"). As played, Ch 12 is the postgame
chapter moved inside the ticking clock (notes E15 raises the same contradiction as a design question).

**Game-wide milestones.** This part sits inside Berger's Act 3 between the **ticking time bomb** (Ch 11) and the
**obligatory scene** (Ch 13). Berger's beat here is "escalating burdens intensify" (p. 85). Avarice's return is a
"new threat appears" escalation by his definition, which is the strongest case for it; but it escalates a *different*
villain's plot two chapters before the final boss, which is the overexposure Berger warns about (p. 88). The
ticking clock set in Ch 11 neither ticks nor is mentioned: no line in the part refers to Frostina, the three hearts,
or any deadline, except Wyn's "She went north. To the island. To Auril." (12.10), which is about Avarice.

**Frostina is never named in Part 12.** Not once in 155 boxes. The heartbeat Alerion said "a Dragoon can follow"
(11.06) is not mentioned on the sea crossing, the one place it would steer the griffons.

**Tone.** "Cosy, then cold" works scene by scene (camp coffee, then Sopo's "into holes"; Tamsin's bluster, then the
fog and the masts). Across the chapter it runs backwards: the coldest thing (Avarice) comes first, and the warmest
(the camp) comes last, the night before the climax, with no cold under it. The camp is the one place in the act
where "light banter that stops dead when the wind picks up" is what the scene needs, and the wind never picks up.

## 7. Game fit

- **The gameplay challenge isn't the story's conflict.** The chapter is named for a sea crossing that has no
  gameplay, while the gameplay (a three-room dungeon and a five-Pokémon boss) belongs to a villain the clock isn't
  about. Despain's rule (via the skill): the story's conflict should match or parallel the gameplay challenge.
  Berger: each location needs an internal reason on the golden path (p. 44). The Vault's reason is "the sigils point
  there", which is context, not story (Berger pp. 20–21).
- **Story as reward.** Strand A rewards play well: the Herald relics, earned in Act II, *do* something here, and the
  cradle hands Meat Meat Razzy's scales. Strand B is a wall: the crossing and the camp are input-free text.
- **Scene/sequel rhythm.** Strand A has scenes (the circuits, the cradle) and then a seven-map walk instead of a
  sequel. The real sequel to Ch 11's disaster (Frostina taken) should be the crossing or the camp: reaction,
  dilemma, decision (Mills p. 108). Neither has a dilemma.
- **Momentum** (Seger p. 79): the seed of Ch 13 is planted only at the very end ("She's expecting you!"). The seed of
  the Vault was planted by a sigil, not by the plot.
- **Meaningful choices** (Berger p. 94): the Wall's "Run together" (outcome + character) and Tamsin's Nod
  (character) pass. Relic-or-wraith at the circuits is close to Berger's gun-vs-knife (p. 99). The relics' yes/no
  passes only on one Ch 13 branch.

## 8. Verdict

**What's working**

1. **Tamsin's goodbye** pays off the player character's arc from the Prologue ("It has no face") at the right
   moment, and gives the silent hero a decision that is pure character: Nod, or Say nothing.
2. **The Herald relics stop the circuits.** Four Act II side quests each get a payoff in the owner's voice ("Let her
   look for herself in the fog"; "Some things should stay locked").
3. **The cradle gives Meat Meat her refusal** ("Meat Meat owes you NOTHING") one chapter before the Test of Cruelty,
   and "Razzy's. From the cold table" is the warmest beat in the part.
4. **The "hold" motif** (12.09, 12.11) begins here and carries the theme into all three endings.

**Top 3 story problems**

1. **The chapter answers the postgame's question, inside the ticking clock.** Avarice's return, her plan, her body
   and her end take the part's whole middle; Frostina is never mentioned. *Why it hurts:* it spends the urgency Ch 11
   built, gives a secondary villain ten boxes of menace two chapters before the final boss (Auril has none here),
   and uses up the postgame hook. *Fix (owner's call, notes E15):* move the Vault back to the postgame. The code
   already has the branch where Avarice comes to the throne without the Vault (13.17, "I had another in a glacier.
   You never came to look."), so Act III loses nothing it needs. If the Vault stays, cut it to one scene and make
   it plainly optional. *Cite:* Berger p. 88 (don't overexpose or overshadow the final villain), p. 44 (golden-path
   logic); Seger p. 75 (a subplot that doesn't push the plot is a detour).
2. **The Sea of Moving Ice has no sea in it.** "Can we reach her?" is answered yes, for free, in eleven boxes of
   narration. *Why it hurts:* the crossing is the act's threshold into the antagonist's world; without an obstacle it doesn't feel like Auril's territory, and the ally you elected does nothing but
   carry you. *Fix:* one obstacle the ally solves in their own way (suggestion: the moving ice closes; Rheven's
   griffons must climb into Auril's weather; Mira threads the masts; Angajuk dives), and Alerion steering by the
   heartbeat ("Left. She's left of the skull."). The bell route already has two sea battles (Walrein, Cloyster); the other routes could get one each.
   *Cite:* Seger p. 82 (an obstacle forces a decision); Mills pp. 85–86 (setting as antagonist).
3. **The night before the climax doesn't carry the central question.** The camp is coffee and drinks. Reptin's "we
   decide what happens to Auril" is not raised again until the choice box. *Why it hurts:* at 13.18 the three
   endings arrive as a menu read aloud by five people in seven boxes. If the party argued them here, the choice
   would be the end of a dilemma the player has slept on. *Fix:* the sketch below. *Cite:* Seger p. 43 (each turning
   point raises the central question again); Mills pp. 108–109 (the sequel's dilemma), p. 129 (choices between two
   rights).

**Rewrite sketch: 12.15, "The night on the dock" (the weakest important scene)**

Owner's lines kept where marked; new material is *(suggestion)*.

1. **Arrival.** The camp sprite matches the route (notes E12). The Heralds are staged round a stove; Frostina's
   heartbeat line from Alerion as you land: "Close. She's up there." *(suggestion)* The clock is back.
2. **Banter (kept).** Meat Meat's "This island has no coffee"; Wyn's mushroom coffee; "Mushroom friend. Meat Meat will
   die for you. Tomorrow, probably."; Alberica: "Nobody's dying tomorrow, sugar. I haven't finished my drink."
   Alerion: "You finished it an hour ago."
3. **The wind picks up.** Alberica's rune glows through her coat; she stops mid-joke (Voices: "Under pressure: the
   jokes stop"). Nobody comments for one box.
4. **Reptin brings the question back** *(suggestion)*: "We said we'd decide on the way. We're out of way."
5. **Opposing scripts** (Mills p. 127), each Herald's position, one or two lines each *(suggestion)*:
   - Meat Meat, Dawnfang across her knees: "One stab. Then nobody has to be a vessel ever again." (Ending A, and the
     temptation her Test will ask her to refuse)
   - Alberica, plain: "If somebody has to hold her, it should be the one she wants." (Ending C)
   - Reptin: "Holding someone and keeping them aren't the same thing, darling." (the motif, said once, before the
     ghost says it)
   - Alerion: "Shimmer didn't break her. She didn't give in. I've been listening for what she did instead." (Ending B)
   - Wyn: no position, only "...Um. Can we not decide in the cold?" (keeps their brave line for 13.18)
6. **The player's decision** *(suggestion)*: "Sit with..." Meat Meat / Alberica / Alerion. One quiet two-line beat
   with that Herald (for example Alberica: "Don't let me be brave the wrong way, sweetheart."). It sets a flag that
   adds that Herald's line, by name, to the choice in 13.18 ("[Name] sat with me last night."). Light branching on
   one character's perspective, as Berger recommends on a budget (pp. 99–100).
7. **Sleep and dawn.** Heal; fade; Sopo on the bollard: "She's expecting you! Everybody's expecting you." The scene
   ends on a hook (Mills p. 152), and the climb begins.
