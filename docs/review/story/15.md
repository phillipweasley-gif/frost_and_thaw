# 15 · Side: Rootspire's quests and townsfolk (Act II) — story review

Scene numbers are from `docs/review/script/15.md` (15.01-15.40). Line, staging and bug findings are in `docs/review/notes/15.md` and are only pointed at here ("notes E5"). Most of this part is *from the code, not played*: the run spent the seven-night downtime week at the Hearth and played the Lorn Scribe's quests, the omens and some vignettes. Scores below are for the version a player would get from the code, which is the only version there is.

The bible's claim for this part (story_bible.md, "Act II personal quests (feed the Trials)"): *"Most Act II side content is now a personal quest that becomes evidence at the Trials of the Roots. Each finished personal quest adds a witness."* The main job of this review is to test that claim, quest by quest.

## 1. The chapter in one line

**Question (as a player would put it from the script):** *What has Rootspire kept for the Heralds, and what is burning under it?* Two questions, really: a personal one ("the tree found your pages", 15.03; "Riddles, Herald? One for each of you.") and a mystery ("Something below is burning the seasons alive", Lirielle, 15.17).

**Answer it gives:** the personal one is answered well, four times, by the Lorn Scribe's relics (15.24): each Herald gets back something of what they were. The mystery is answered too early and too often. By the end of the downtime week a player who talks to people has been told that Summer is "a woman. A tiefling" with horns and no face (Razzilith, 15.06; Maera's robes, 15.25), that her mark is a cracked mirror round an A (Harget, 15.18; the Bazaar's mirrors, 15.16), and that she is building a chardalyn dragon at a forge under the old town (Glimmer's vision, 15.12; the Reflection night, 15.09). That is the Chapter 8 reveal, given away in optional content before Chapter 7 opens.

**Does the game say the question?** The mystery, yes, on the spine: *"KELYN: And a season too long. Thirty years of summer. I want to know why."* (the hook for Kethan, 15.20). The personal question, only as the Scribe's riddle line. The question this part is *supposed* to serve, "who will speak for you when the city judges you?", is never asked here. Every personal quest in the downtime week happens before the arrest (Ch10 beat 1), so nobody can say "this will count", and nobody does.

**Does it answer a question the last chapter raised?** Chapter 6 ends with "find the Summer Marshal, and stop what burns below" (bible Ch6 beat 11). The side content answers "who is Summer" before the spine has asked it properly, and with three different answers (section 5, "Summer"). The bible says Act II side content should feed the Ch10 question "what did the Heralds do, and were they right?" As played, it mostly feeds the Ch8 question, and spoils it.

## 2b. The personal quests as subplots (Seger pp. 63-76) and whether they feed the Trials

There is no `witnesses` counter that "each finished personal quest" adds to, as the bible's Balance notes say.

**Result.** Of the part's personal quests, the Trials read four: the Oath of Roots, the Heist, Maera's robes (and her apprentice) and Maera's weasel. Three of them are Autumn evidence for Meat Meat's charge. **None of the four Lorn Scribe relic quests, the ones that match the bible's Herald personal quests most closely, feeds the trial, and nor do the Still Ones, Kethan, Eren, Arvik, the Library week or Glimmer's rite.** The bible's witnesses for them (Rheven for the staff, Talion for the mantle, Mira for the blade, Eirwen for the Canticle, the Still Ones) are either set somewhere else or don't exist. Most also happen in Chapter 6, when the trial is three chapters away and unannounced. Seger's test is that a subplot "pushes and intersects the plot" near its turning points (p. 64, 67); these intersect the postgame (the White Vault reads the relics, 047c:501-537) and, for the Canticle, the final battle, but not the Act II climax they were moved here to feed.

**The player character.** Decisions in this part: whether to go with Meat Meat each morning; whom to ask about the lanterns; how to treat Tobin (three ways); lose or face the grey cloak; the emissary's question; save or leave the yeti cub; hold the reflection's gaze; Arvik's step choices; Eren's choice; Reptin's answer to Rheven; the Oath; help, shatter or leave each Still One; how to research and sing the Canticle; weapon or seed; how to sit the staff night; hope or judgment; Kelyn's relic; Naerth's ledger; the reliquary's fate; Halden; Wynsnow; the petitions; plus a dozen battles you can lose (Ash, Eira, the Still Beast, the Crypt Guardian, the Rime Stalker, the Sliver, the Garment-Double). And the open city: you choose where to go.

Done *to* you: the 57-box first morning, the rumours, fifteen omens, the vignettes, the night deck, the Scribe's 18 boxes, Glimmer's 41.

**Active or passenger?** Active in *where*, passenger in *who*. Almost every quest waits for a particular Herald (*"Whatever the staff is waiting for, it isn't you."*), and most of the big choices are a Herald's lines spoken through you (*"What does Reptin tell him?"*, *"Weapon, or seed?"*). That fits the bible ("your choices are your voice"), but the choices that carry the theme are read by nothing: `blade_seed`, `glimmer_hope/_judgment`, `rheven_mercy/_balance/_together`, `naerth_ledger_*`, `kelyn_warned`. Berger's test is that a choice changes the outcome, moves the plot or reveals something (p. 94); these reveal something, once, and then the game forgets. The Oath of Roots goes further: a wrong answer loops back until you pick "Both", which is a back-out choice (Berger p. 94). New Journey breaks the player's position twice: Halden sends you "back to your origin. Targos", and Eirwen's ritual says *"Faces you remember"* to a child born in Rootspire (notes E18).

**Tone.** "Cosy, then cold" mostly holds scene by scene (Tavern Revelry, then the Reflection night). Across the week it doesn't: so many townsfolk end on a premonition that the cosy never settles and the cold never lands as a change. "Light banter that stops dead when the wind picks up" needs calm wind first. "Sad moments get one quiet beat and no speeches": Rilka's scene does exactly this; Glimmer's 41-box acceptance is the opposite.

## 7. Game fit

- **Reward or wall?** The relic quests are story as reward done right: a riddle, a place, a fight or a puzzle, then a personal scene and a held item (Berger p. 35). The downtime mornings are walls: 57 boxes before the first morning's input, 18 for the Scribe, 41 for Glimmer's last day.
- **Scene/sequel (Mills p. 108).** Where a battle is the disaster, the sequel is missing: knocking out the Rime Stalker (the wrong result) replays the whole setup instead of a reaction, and losing to the Verdant Surge has no consequence. The Heist is the model: the wards are goal/conflict, the reliquary's whisper is the disaster, the Council choice is the decision.
- **Does the conflict match the challenge?** Twice, cleverly: the Still Beast and the Rime Stalker must be *caught, not killed* (*"Winter's cruelty, held still... It's harder than killing."*), which is the creed as a mechanic. Elsewhere the challenge is a trainer battle unrelated to the problem (Halden's reset, the revenants as "Sailor" trainers).
- **Meaningful choices.** Many choices reveal character; few change anything (section 4). The petitions can't be lost if you hold any finding, and the heist wards' right answer is always the first option (notes E7).
- **Delivery.** Spine-level reveals (Summer's identity, Frostina's ritual, the Rite of the Long Gold) sit in optional second talks with no signpost, against "never hide spine information in optional-looking dialogue" (IGDA p. 120) and "layer by relevance" (IGDA p. 109). How players find any of it is notes E1-E3.

## 8. Verdict

**What's working**
1. The Lorn Scribe's relic quests are true personal quests: each gives a Herald a short, specific scene about who they were (the V-7 page, Talion's name, the wolf totem, the song "I haven't written yet") and a choice that states their arc.
2. The Canticle is a model subplot: set up in Act II, chosen by the player in the theme's own words, paid off in the final battle.
3. The Verdant Heist is the one quest that crosses the main plot three times (the Forge, the trial, the voyage), and its choice matters.
4. The omens are a real escalating throughline with a payoff that hurts (no. 13), and the evolving gold dawn is a motif that tracks the whole act.

**Top 3 story problems**

1. **The personal quests don't feed the Trials.** *Why it hurts:* the bible moved optional content into personal quests precisely so they would matter at the Act II climax; as built, the Verdict reads four of them, all for one charge, and none of the Herald relic quests. The player's best side work earns held items and postgame doors, not a defence. Most happen three chapters before anyone says "trial". *Fix:* add the Herald quests to `WITNESSES` under the charges they suit (staff and Oath → `:reptin`/`:summer`; mantle → feeds Talion's echo; blade → `:autumn`; Canticle → Eirwen; Still Ones → `:summer`), make the Verdict count the `:reptin`, `:wyn` and `:spores` lists it already shows, and seed the trial in Chapter 6 (sketch below). Subplots must intersect the plot near its turning points (Seger pp. 64, 67, 75).
2. **The side content spoils and muddles the Act II mystery.** *Why it hurts:* "Summer is a horned woman with an A" is told in at least six optional scenes before Chapter 8's reveal, while Helion Vael and the Rite of the Long Gold give two rival origins for the summer that the bible doesn't have. Ten seers and both captains deliver the same warning. A twist must be implied, not stated (Seger pp. 86-88); repetition past three becomes noise (Seger p. 83); twelve characters with one function blur (Seger pp. 261-262). *Fix:* keep three hints on the path (Razzilith's "no one ever sees Summer's face", Maera's horn-slits, Harget's A) and cut the word "tiefling" and the white-haired woman before Ch8; *(suggestion)* fold Helion into Var (the Suncleft was Var's shop) and make the Rite of the Long Gold the ritual Avarice hijacked as Var; merge the seers into three (Lirielle for scent, Ysera for souls, Yorin for song).
3. **The Herald beats happen without cost, and the decisions that carry them are forgotten.** *Why it hurts:* Reptin's near-god beat is all warmth and no fear; Alerion's Talion beat is split between two scenes that lock each other out and is missing from his own week; Meat Meat's seed/weapon and Glimmer's hope/judgment are read by nothing. A transformation needs beats the player can see change something (Seger pp. 255-258), and a choice that changes nothing is cosmetic (Berger pp. 94, 99). *Fix:* give the reverence a cost (a Rootwarden initiate who would follow Reptin into the fire, and Arvik's line "They'd do anything you said. That's what frightens me."); give Krolf his own flag and make the hunter follow it; read `blade_seed` in the Test of Cruelty and `glimmer_hope` in the Forge's last lines.

**Rewrite sketch: the Lorn Scribe (15.03), the gate to all four personal quests.** Now: 18 boxes, four riddles at once, Alberica's Codex folded in, no input, never repeated, no link to the Trials. *(Suggestions are marked; names and voices are the owner's.)*

1. **The Library, first visit with any Herald.** The Scribe writes without looking up. The player talks to him. One line about who he writes for (*"The tree. So it remembers when it wakes."*). He stops writing and looks at the Herald in front, not at the party.
2. **One riddle, for that Herald only.** With Meat Meat in front: *"The blade sleeps in the silt where the boats were frozen."* Meat Meat answers in her own voice (*"Silt. Meat Meat knows silt."*). The riddle goes into a side-quest line under the Goal (notes E2).
3. **He says what the pages are for** *(suggestion)*: *"The tree writes down who you were. One day, someone will have to read it aloud."* This plants the trial without naming it.
4. **The player chooses** whether to bring the Herald back later or leave it (a two-option ask). Leaving it costs nothing; the Scribe says *"Come back when the blade's found."*
5. **On each return with a found relic**, the Scribe writes one line of the quest's outcome into his book, read on screen (*"V-7 chose: seed."*), and gives the next Herald's riddle if that Herald is present.
6. **Alberica's turn is last.** Only after the other three, *"Keeper of Memory"*: the journal pages, and the Codex morning split over two rests (notes E14).
7. **At the Trials (Ch10)** *(suggestion)*: the Scribe comes to the Council Hall with the book, and each line he wrote is a witness for its Herald's charge. One new `WITNESSES` entry per relic flag, spoken in his voice.
8. **What changes:** the four quests become one visible chain with a goal, a recurring giver and a payoff at the Act II climax; the player decides when each starts; the Scribe gains a function (the tree's memory) that no other NPC has.
