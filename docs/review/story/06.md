# 06 · Chapter 6: The Festival of Returning Light (story review)

*Judged against the game-narrative-writing skill (Seger, Mills, Berger). Scene numbers and quotes are from `docs/review/script/06.md` (spine only; Rootspire's side content is part 15). The design is `story_bible.md`, "Chapter 6". Line, staging and bug findings are in `docs/review/notes/06.md` and are cited by E-, F- or D-number, not repeated.*

## 1. The chapter in one line

**As the player would put it:** *Where are we, and what's wrong with this city?* **Four hundred years on, inside a bubble of the Reversal's time. The Summer Marshal is missing, his decree carries the hag's cracked mirror, the Halo gave him Sephek's heart, and something under the tree is hammering. Go down and find him.**

**Does the game say the question?** The bible's question, "Where, and when, are we?", is the first Goal ("The Rooted Hearth. Hellen will know what happened.") and Tamsin's first line ("WHERE DID YOU GO. You were gone an HOUR."). **It is answered in the second scene**: "The tree grew in one night that lasted four hundred years. Inside the Auroral Wall, anyway." In fact Chapter 5 answered it before the chapter started ("Four hundred years, all at once."; the panel of "Your own face, looking in").

**What the chapter actually asks**, from 6.04 on, is "Where is the Summer Marshal, and what's burning under the tree?" Nobody puts that as a question until Kelyn's summons in the last scene: "Find our Summer Marshal. And stop whatever is burning the roots." (6.25). That's the bible's Chapter 7 question ("What is burning under the tree?"). So Chapter 6 answers the question Chapter 5 raised in two scenes, then spends twenty-four raising the next chapter's. That would be fine for a set-up chapter if the Act II mission came early. It comes at the end (problem 1).

**The handover from Chapter 5 is excellent.** Thredra's last words, "Mirrors always remember.", are picked up in the Council: "A decree... In the wax there's a tiny mark: a cracked mirror." / "MEAT MEAT: Mirrors remember. She said so." The twins' "WAIT" in the fall becomes Razzilith's "...WAITED." The act break has its seeds on both sides.

**Gameplay vs text.** Gameplay carries TP1 (Eline), the climax (the festival hunt) and the Halo at the orb. Text carries the opening, the catalyst, the midpoint and both halves of TP2. The Rift transformation, the chapter's showpiece, is 56 boxes with one effect (E7).

**Game-wide milestones in this part.**

- **Berger's "adjustment to the new world"**, the first beat of Act 2 (p. 82). Chapter 6 is exactly that, and does it well: Hellen feeds you, your own statues stand in the Council Hall, and the city has "Two weathers, one street."
- **Berger's point of no return (p. 81–82): spread across three scenes, and none of them does the whole job.** (a) The Reversal (5.27) is the physical point of no return, but it happens *to* the party. (b) The Wardens' oath (6.04) is the right moment, a choice that commits the heroes to Rootspire, but it's cosmetic: `wardens_emeritus` is set either way and only two lines read `wardens_refused` (E27). The bible says "A 'no' changes Kelyn's trust"; as played, it doesn't. (c) The summons (6.25) states the mission, but Kelyn gives it, and it gets one line of assent ("ALERION: Then that's where we go."). Mills's first doorway is a threshold the hero *chooses* to cross, with no way back (p. 29, 112). Here nobody chooses.
- **Seger's mission rule**: by the start of Act Two the audience should know roughly what the hero plans to do (p. 46). The plan arrives in 6.25, the last scene. Until then the Goal is festival errands and "Rest at the Hearth" (problem 1).
- **A false ticking clock.** The festival countdown ("6 more nights, love. Nobody rushes the seventh night.") is a clock with no threat on it, advanced by resting. Selrae's and Eline's "tonight" stays tonight while the player burns nights (E12). Mills's ticking clock needs a cost for waiting (p. 192).

**Done to them:** the arrival; Hellen's explanation; the dragons' landing; Brightmane's summons; the Codex; the omens; the shudder; the summons; Wyn joining.

**Verdict: free in space, a passenger in the story.** The hub lets the player pick the order of things, and the festival hunt is a real test with a real cost. But both spine choices (the oath, Shade's "Go") are empty, and the festival week is "Rest at the Hearth" six times, with yes/no prompts to watch Meat Meat do things. We're entering Act II, which is where Seger says the protagonist must start acting on the story (p. 232, 251). The player is about to be told what to do by a Council, and wasn't asked whether to go home.

## 7. Game fit

- **Story as a wall.** The Council is 62 boxes with one choice; the orb about 60, 44 of them unbroken; the Rift 56; night 2 50; festival night about 100 (E10). Berger: never push a player into lean-back mode inside a lean-forward game (p. 45, 150). For a hub chapter, much of this should be found in play, not delivered.
- **Scene and sequel.** Chapter 6 is one long sequel to Act I's disaster: reaction (the Wall, the legends), dilemma, decision (Mills, p. 108). **The dilemma is missing.** Five people have lost their time, and one of them a husband, and nobody asks "Can we go back?" The answer is barely in the script (only in the Echo Gate's decline branch: "ALERION: Weeks out there. Four hundred years in here. I'm not walking through to find out."). Without the dilemma, the decision (the oath) has nothing to decide.
- **The conflict matches the gameplay, where there is gameplay.** The festival hunt turns your earlier choices into tools (the apprentice's tip, Summer-Sense), and failure costs lives that the Council remembers the next morning. That's the best narrative design in Act II so far: Berger's "story responds to gameplay results" (p. 111–112), and light branching with a real outcome (p. 100). The cellar gym's "Show me I wasn't lying." makes a gym fight about the legend being true, which is the player's own fan-to-peer arc as a battle.
- **Choices.** The festival steps and the lantern rumour are meaningful. The oath, "Go." / "Come back." and Eline's offer are cosmetic (Berger, p. 99). The "Go with her?" prompts are optional cutscenes with a yes/no in front. A rest-to-advance week is fine for a hub, but with no threat on the clock it drains the act turn's urgency (§2).

## 8. Verdict

**What's working.**

1. **The act break is stitched on both sides.** "Mirrors always remember." → the cracked-mirror seal; "WAIT" → "...WAITED."; the horn, Luna's spark and the journal all pay at the Rift.
2. **The theme gets its clearest statement in the game so far**: "Nothing that is still can grieve." / "I'd rather grieve. It means I had something."
3. **The festival hunt** is narrative design: your choices from earlier nights become tools, and failure has a body count the city remembers.
4. **Shown characterisation in small actions**: Kelyn's white hand, Meat Meat's hands in her pockets, Shade walking out on the bard.

**Top 3 story problems.**

**1. The act turn commits nobody to anything, and Act II has no mission until the last scene.** *Why it hurts:* the Reversal happens to the party, the oath changes nothing, and the mission is handed down by Kelyn in 6.25. Five people cut off from their time never consider going home; Reptin's husband gets one line. So the first stretch of Act II is a week of rests with a clock that threatens nothing. Seger: the plan must be clear by the start of Act Two, or the audience watches passively (p. 46), and a gap between turning points leaves them in limbo (p. 47–48). Berger: the point of no return is the hero's first heroic act committing them (p. 81–82). Mills: the first doorway is a threshold *chosen* (p. 112). *Fix:* make the Council scene the point of no return, a real choice between going home through the Wall and staying to guard the pulse, with a mission and a clock attached. See the sketch below. Make "no" change Kelyn's trust, as the bible says, and read it at the Trials.

**2. Shade becoming Glimmer isn't Shade's choice, and happens off stage.** *Why it hurts:* Act I's best foreshadowing (the horn, the cat, Luna, the journal, "Glimmer. That name again.") arrives at a transformation the player never sees and Shade never decides. Shade's time in Rootspire is a few quips, a walk-out and one "...Hi." Then they leave their newly hatched sister for seven years because "Shimmer told me to keep going.", and the player's "Go." / "Come back." does nothing.
