# Part 14 · Story review: the Ten-Towns quests and Act I's optional townsfolk

Scope as in `docs/review/script/14.md`: Mountain Climb, Foaming Mugs, the Council of Speakers, A Beautiful Mine, The White Moose, The Mead Must Flow with the Good Mead election, the rumour table, and the optional people of Targos, Bryn Shander, the shore road, Termalaine, Lonelywood, Good Mead and Dougan's Hole. The run reached almost none of it, so "as played" here means the script written from the code, which is what a player who finds it gets. Line, staging and bug findings are in `notes/14.md` and are only pointed to here.

**One finding before the rest: the design and the build disagree about whether this part exists.** The bible's Act I quest table ("Quests and side content / Act I") lists none of the five big quests built here. Its cut list ("Systemic text / Cut list") names three of the six sequences: "The white moose; Ramshackle's hanging; the Lucky Liar's spy" and "Shandar Froth's election and the verbeeg in Good Mead". Its first table says why: "Good Mead, the white moose and Ramshackle lead nowhere." The build has all of it anyway, about 1,270 boxes, finished and branching. Act II pays Good Mead off only through the spine's Olivessa (`good_mead_met`, Ch9), never through this content. The owner needs to choose. This review argues for keeping it and tying it in (section 8), because it is the best "small warmth against a vast cold" material in the game, but as built the bible's verdict is correct: it leads nowhere.

## 1. The part in one line

**The question, as a player would put it:** what is the Rime doing to ordinary people in Ten-Towns, and does it matter that we stopped to help?

**The answer the script gives:** it is killing them in small, specific ways (a husband on a mountain, a speaker speared for his mead, a mine shut, loggers hunted by a moose that thinks), and helping matters a great deal, but only inside the town where you helped. Nobody in another town, no Herald at a later rest and no spine scene ever mentions it (notes E14).

**Does the game ever say the question?** Once, in the one scene most players will miss. At the Council (14.07), Duvessa says the charter ends "If She were appeased. / Or dealt with some other way.", Reptin asks what "some other way" would look like, and Oarus answers: "Like somebody walking out of that door and doing something none of us can." Kendrick raises his mug: "Like that, maybe." That is the part's question said aloud. Nothing points the player to the Council, and it expires once you reach Termalaine or Lonelywood (notes E8).

**Does it answer a question the last chapter raised?** The bible gives side content no question of its own, since it was designed out. As built, the quests answer a question the spine never asks: Act I's spine asks "who called us, and what does Auril want?" (Ch1–Ch4 rows of the chapter table), and no quest here touches the relics, the vessel rule or the hag. What they answer is the bible's tone pillar, "Towns, gyms and side quests are about people holding on". They answer it well, but nothing in the A story turns on the answer.

**The Heralds.** No bible arc beat *lands* here; four are *rhymed*, which is the right job for side content (Seger, p. 63).

- **Reptin** swears four oaths: "That's a promise, not a pleasantry.", "We swear it. All three.", the swearing again at Keegan's door, and "I swear it." to Norsu. That is a motif feeding his bible line "I'll keep it. The wolf. The watch." He shares the comforter function with Alberica ("Up you come, love." / "Rest now, friend.").
- **Alerion**'s Frostbound Ring (set up in Parts 00–01) goes cold near the dead, four times, and finds Janth. He and Shade share the tracker function. "Panic doesn't walk. It climbs." is unmistakably his; "We take the dwarf home." could be anyone's.
- **Shimmer** hears her own death coming at Marta's song ("A woman running from the wind." / "...I know how that one ends."). That is the best disguised setup here (Seger, p. 148). The script doesn't say whether her Cairn and mine lines are gated after Ch3 ("Gone means gone"). Check.
- **Meat Meat**: "Meat Meat was cold like goblins once. Very cold. In a cage." This is an early, earned piece of her Act II arc.
- **Shade**: "Somebody's pretending to be something they aren't. / I'd know." It could seed Glimmer; nothing builds on it.

**The player character.** The player makes about thirty decisions: take or refuse each quest, the dogs, the avalanche, Garret home or with you, the yeti mother, Oobok's remains, the blizzard, the goblins (three ways) and Izobai, Scramsax, Marta's ring, the saboteurs, the grell, Trex (three ways) and the satchel, the kobolds' fate, Nimsy's fee, the fox, the mirror's seven questions, the chwinga, Friend, the goats, the chieftain's pearl, Gahg (four ways), the speakership, the thug. Done *to* them: Kendrick's, Mokingo's and Astrix's deaths, and the staged rescue. **The player is more active here than anywhere on Act I's spine.** Most choices pass Berger's test (p. 94): the grell and the satchel change the ending, the yeti mother keeps a family, Gahg's fate changes Good Mead's nights. "Your choices are your voice" (bible) is truest here. The exceptions are the pitches (a false choice, Berger p. 36) and Nimsy's fee, where generosity pays less (notes E32).

## 6. Theme and tone

- **Small warmth against a vast cold.** This is the pillar the part was built for, and it delivers: Keegan's cider, the walrus with "BIG LOVE" carved into its side, six goblins under one blanket "like a very lumpy tent", Good Mead asleep in a heap round the hive, Marta's ring "warm as a hand", Southwell's "First time I didn't have to drag anybody to the post."
- **Every mirror lies a little.** The Mirror of the Full Moon shows the truth and hides the rest ("The glass frosts over before it shows you more."). Janth wears Trex, then Oarus. Trex wears fake dragon wings. Shandar stages a rescue that Shade reads as "a play" (a façade, Mills p. 130). Each reveal costs someone something, as the bible asks.
- **Change over stillness (the foundation).** Janth's "If they go, I go." and then "That's much lighter." is the whole theme in two lines, in an optional mine. Astrix, frozen staring at the boots, is stillness as death. Ravisin is grief that hardened into hate, Auril's motive in miniature. The charter's "necessary evil" is the towns choosing stillness.
- **Found family across time.** Absent. Nothing here crosses the 400 years except Olivessa in Ch9 and the hidden Wheel. Found family *within* the time is strong (Keegan and Garret, Duhg and Gahg and their named goats, Perilou and Mokingo), which is why section 8's fix carries it across.
- **Serves no pillar:** the three pitches, the shore road, Triglio, the tourmaline chipping, the skull and geodes, the verbeeg's baskets, Sahnar, and the four dead rumours. These are the cut candidates.

**Tone.** "Cosy, then cold" is right almost everywhere: the cider, then the heads in the walls; the goats with names, then "That isn't pork." The bible's rule "Sad moments get one quiet beat and no speeches" is kept for Astrix and Gahg. Three breaks: Nimsy's cookie ask the morning Shimmer is taken; Alberica's sarcasm over Oobok's remains (14.05-4); and the mummy that "smells like an autumn latte", a modern joke in a fairy-tale tomb.

**Berger and DeMarle on optional content.** The rule (DeMarle in IGDA, p. 109; Bateman, p. 120) is to rate each story detail by relevance, keep critical beats on the spine and leave icing in optional content.

- **Nothing plot-critical is hidden here.** That is good. The nearest miss is the Council, where the towns' answer to Alberica's stand and the charter's escape clause sit at relevance 2–3, behind a door with no breadcrumb that expires. Put Duvessa's two lines ("We'd end it in a heartbeat ... Or dealt with some other way.") into the spine's Ch2 exit, and keep the Council as the long version.
- **The reverse problem is the real one: the theme's best statements are all optional.** Janth's satchel, Ravisin's grief and the charter say the bible's foundation more clearly than most spine scenes do, and a golden-path player sees none of them. The foundation needs one spine echo of each (section 8).
- **The mirror previews Act III** (Auril's island, Sephek's nature). That's acceptable as icing, but it must not contradict the spine ("a fine coat"; "a castle that is also a cloud" against the crowned-skull Grimskalle). A twist or preview must never contradict what's established (Seger, p. 87).
- **Breadcrumbs** (Bateman, pp. 116–120). The rumour table is the part's breadcrumb system, and it leads with Bremen, a town that isn't in the game (notes E6).

**Story as reward, and rhythm.** The quests are rewards, not walls, apart from four long lean-back runs: Keegan (17 boxes), the Council (27), Marta (17) and the tomb's five map-describing boxes (Berger, p. 45). Scene and sequel are well paired: the crag-cat fight leads into Garret's list and a choice; Duhg's fall leads into Gahg's return and a choice (Mills, p. 108). Conflict matches challenge: on the Cairn the cold is an HP drain; in the mine the possession is a ghost battle. The moose hunt is the exception, where "It finds them" plays as a random grind. **Choices are meaningful** by Berger's test (p. 94), more than anywhere else in Act I, and they fail DeMarle's "acknowledge small actions" test (pp. 105–106) as soon as you leave the town.

## 8. Verdict

**What's working**

1. **Two complete subplots.** Mountain Climb and the Mine each have a hook, a commitment, a turn, a climax and a resolution. The grell is a textbook complication: a choice in the dark pays off a day later in "Termalaine has no speaker." (Seger, pp. 84, 90–93).
2. **The player decides.** About thirty real choices. The silent protagonist's "choices are your voice" is truest here.
3. **Small warmth, and the offering motif.** Five or more images make the Rime's cost concrete.
4. **Quests that cross each other.** Oobok joins two quests, the yetis three, and the Ch5 Yetot turns the father yeti aside.

**Top 3 story problems**

**1. The quests never cross the A story, and the design has cut half of them.** *Why it hurts:* a subplot must push and intersect the plot, not float free as a detour (Seger, pp. 63, 75). These are detours, so the bible's "lead nowhere" is fair. Cutting them, though, loses Act I's best warmth. *Fix:* the owner chooses keep or cut. If keep, give each quest **one** crossing (all suggestions):
- **Mountain Climb:** Keegan and Garret are among Ch9's Bryn Shander refugees. The bible already puts the widow's husband there.
- **Foaming Mugs:** "Ye've friends under the mountain now. ... We won't." pays off when the Battlehammer dwarves man the walls in Ch9.
- **Mine:** when Var Rime-Breaker turns out to be Avarice at the Forge, Alerion says: "Termalaine. My ring went this cold for Oarus." That is a setup in one context paid off in another (Seger, p. 148).
- **White Moose:** Ravisin names the Frostmaiden before the fight. The mirror's Sephek answer hints at what rides him. *(Suggestion; check the timeline: the bible's "frost druid's spirit" in Sephek could be Vurnis.)*
- **Mead and election:** Ch9 reads `mead_returned` and the vote. Speaker Olivessa, or you, or Shandar opening Bryn Shander's gate late on Naerth's word.
- **Council:** put it on the spine (section 7).
- **Across all of them:** the Trials of the Roots already count witnesses, and the refugees are in the gallery. One Ten-Towns refugee per finished Act I quest (Keegan, Hruna, Oarus or Shaelen, Nimsy, Olivessa) could stand up for the Heralds and add a witness. That is Seger's subplot climaxing near the plot's climax (pp. 67, 75), using a system the game already has.

**2. The world forgets, so the theme's best moments never reach the story.** *Why it hurts:* DeMarle's immediate-level story ("what I did on the Cairn") never meets the high-level one (pp. 104–106). Berger asks that the story respond to the player (pp. 111–112). The six "+1 Wheel" mercies feed an ending scene the player can't trace back to them. *Fix:* notes E14 has the town-level lines. At story level, **let 400 years remember**, which also supplies the missing "found family across time" pillar. *(Suggestion.)* Rootspire's legends say the Heralds "talked a yeti mother down" and "brought the Battlehammer iron home". Keegan's walrus sits in Eirwen's vault, its "BIG LOVE" worn smooth. The Wheel's epilogue scene names one mercy the player chose.

**3. The White Moose is in the wrong place, sags, and its mirror contradicts the canon.** *Why it hurts:* the ask is a sequel whose feeling doesn't match the scene it follows (Mills, pp. 108–109). A cookie-plate bounty straight after "Shimmer's bedroll is empty." undercuts Act I's most urgent stakes. The hunt repeats one obstacle about twelve times (Seger, p. 83). The "fine coat" and the cloud-castle break what the spine has established (Seger, p. 87). *Fix:* the sketch below.

**Rewrite sketch: The White Moose, the ask to the mirror** (14.12–14.12-4, the weakest important sequence). Names and voices are the owner's; new lines are suggestions.

1. **First visit** (Nimsy, the party). After "nobody asks about anybody's past", one seed line and no ask: "Mind the logging road. We've a moose problem." The forest logger adds: "Speaker Huddle's paying for it."
