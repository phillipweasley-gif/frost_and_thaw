## 1. The chapter in one line

**As the player would put it:** *Can we reach Avarice's engine before her dragon flies?* **The answer:** *Yes. The engine dies, but it costs Glimmer, Avarice gets away, and the dragon flies anyway.*

**Is the question said?** Not as a question. The clock is said: "Tomorrow, the dragon flies. Tonight, I will burn a path to godhood." / "MEAT MEAT: Tonight. She wrote TONIGHT." / "ALERION: Then it's tonight." (8.17). So is the mission: "Pull it. Go down. End her." (8.04).

**The bible's question, "Who is Summer?", is never asked here, because the game has already answered it.** The Ch6 summons is "Find our Summer Marshal." (6.25). Before Ch8 opens, the player has been told:
- **On the golden path** (Road B, needed for the Root-Key): "SELRAE: I didn't know it was her. / ALBERICA *(very plainly)*: Avarice." (7B.12)
- **Optionally:**
  - "RAZZILITH: Summer is a woman. A tiefling." (6.17)
  - "ATTENDANT: Summer has been wearing a man for thirty years." (7B.11)
  - The liquid-mirror vision: "AVARICE: I need a coat, Marshal." / "VAR: She's wearing me, isn't she." (7B.32)
  - The Scriptory pages: "Summer Marshal Var Rime-Breaker: ... The city trusts his face. Perfect." (7B.34)

So Ch8 does answer a question Ch7 left open, but it's "can we stop it?", not "who is it?". Ch7 already answered its own "what is burning under the tree?" from the walls (7B.28-7B.34). See §5 and Problem 1.

**Gameplay carries:** the gate, about 30 trainers, the root-keep wall, the Breaking Circle, and the Avarice and Shard fights. **Text carries** every beat that matters.

**Game-wide milestone: the midpoint/low point (Berger pp. 83-84, 134-136). It's here, and well placed.** Act II is Ch6-Ch10, so Ch8 is its centre. Before it, Act II is smooth (festival, gyms, roads); after it, it's troubled (dragon, arrest, trials). That's Seger's midpoint shape (pp. 49-50). Fortunes reverse twice in one run: the summer dies, then Glimmer goes, the dragon flies and Avarice escapes. Berger asks that the low point come from the hero's own choice. The game half does this: the engine's death launches the dragon (8.24), and Meat Meat's choice at the bridge is what the city will try her for. Neither is the *player's* choice (§4). The **ticking clock** here ("Tonight", 8.17) is chapter-scale, and the gameplay ignores it.

## 5. Setups and payoffs (Seger pp. 148-161)

### Is "the Summer Marshal is Avarice" set up? (Seger pp. 86-88)

Seger's twist must (1) not contradict what was shown, and (2) overturn an expectation the audience formed itself, down a path it was *invited*, never *forced*, down (p. 87).

| 6.17 | "RAZZILITH: Summer is a woman. A tiefling." / "No one ever sees Summer's face." | One yes | **States it**, and contradicts |

**Contradictions (fix these whatever you choose):**
1. **Razzilith (6.17):** "No one sees Summer... not for many cycles." That clashes with Darrus's "Seven days ago" and with "the city trusts his face" / "Thirty years of my face, smiling at Kelyn" (7B.32). *Fix:* "RAZZILITH: Summer smells wrong. Like the cold table."
2. **Grik (8.11)** describes Avarice's real face ("White hair. Red-gold eyes."), which nobody has seen (notes E20).
3. **Kelyn (8.19):** "Var? Var, thank the seasons..." With `selrae_home` she has already said "Selrae told us. All of it." (7B.12, code). *Fix:* give Selrae a reason to have withheld it, or make Kelyn's line fear: "Var...? Tell me Selrae was wrong."

**Verdict: set up fairly, then over-told.** Nothing misleads the player. But no wrong path (Var as hostage, Var gone mad) is ever offered long enough to walk down. The answer is stated on the golden path once, and up to four more times for curious players. At 8.19 the player is waiting for the party to catch up. So "Summer is Avarice" plays as **a confirmation, not a twist**. The chapter's real twist is a different one, and it's clean: **killing the engine is what launches the dragon** (8.21 "Kill me and it blows" → 8.24).

**Choose one fix; don't mix them.**
- **(a) A twist.** Keep the implications and pull the statements:
  - Selrae says "I didn't know *what* she was."
  - Razzilith smells something, as above.
  - The liquid mirror stays sealed ("Not yet. Not while they're watching" is already its locked text) until the engine dies, then opens in the trial week. `var_story_known` already counts as Ch10 evidence ("A good man, worn like a coat for thirty years.", `036_Chapter10.rb:138`), so Var's echo becomes the defence's witness: two payoffs for one scene.
- **(b) Dramatic irony on purpose** (Seger pp. 101-102). The player knows and the city doesn't, so 8.19's job is cost, not surprise. Rename the chapter's question "Can we reach her before it flies?"

## 7. Game fit

- **Reward or wall?** The upper rooms reward exploring (journals, relics, Bram). The core is a wall: 8.19-8.24 is about 140 boxes around five fights with one heal (notes E8, E11). Berger: never push lean-back into lean-forward (p. 45).
- **Scene/sequel (Mills pp. 108-109).** The dungeon is a fight, then a one-line reaction ("It's quieter when I lose."), which is right. Glimmer's death is a disaster with **no sequel**: it goes straight into a boss and three nested choices that belong to another man's story.
- **Conflict vs gameplay.**
  - Best: **the Breaking Circle.** Avarice writes over Auril and you scrape her off. Every spire is a vote between two goddesses, and Auril answers at the fourth. Story and mechanic are one (Walsh in Despain).
  - Worst: **the root-keep.** A level wall the story doesn't know exists.
- **Choices.** Thredra, the shrine, the spires and the heart change outcomes (Berger p. 94). Two problems: the heart's sub-choices each hide one right answer, so a wrong pick costs the blessing unexplained (a false choice, p. 36). And the option meanings and the tree's prophecy live in optional examinables. Never hide the spine in optional-looking content (IGDA via the skill).

## 8. Verdict

**Working**
1. **A midpoint in the right place that turns hard.** Victory and disaster in one run, and the heroes' own win launches the dragon: cause and effect, not chases (Seger pp. 79, 85-86).
2. **Glimmer's death is earned.** 6.08 → 8.14 → 8.21 → 9.03.
3. **The pens and Autumn falls.** Meat Meat's motive is an action in the present. "We'll talk about that later." / "Yes." does a scene's work in two lines.
4. **The Breaking Circle**, and the coat motif.

**Top 3 problems**

1. **The reveal is spent before it happens.**
   - *Why it hurts:* the chapter's question and its biggest scene rest on a surprise the golden path gave away in Ch7, and three lines contradict it.
   - *Fix:* twist or irony (§5); fix Grik, Razzilith and Kelyn either way.
   - *Cite:* Seger pp. 86-88; Mills p. 123.
2. **The player watches the three defining beats.**
   - *Why it hurts:* it's the midpoint (Seger p. 232), and the catharsis should be the protagonist's (Berger p. 111).
   - *Fix* [S], keeping every outcome:
     - At the bridge, "Grab the cocoon" / "Shout her name", recorded for the trial.
     - At "Your move.", a real move: Strike / Walk away / Look to Glimmer. The first two get one box each and loop back.
     - While he burns, "Take his hand" / "Hum the four notes" / "Say nothing", read at the Shrine.
   - *Cite:* Seger p. 251; IGDA, via the skill (small acknowledgments create agency).
3. **No sequel for the climax, and no case for the villain.**
   - *Why it hurts:* Avarice stands idle through Glimmer's death and the heart choice, then announces her return as the party pronounces her dead. "He likes his meals warm" breaks the unity of opposites (Berger p. 69). In the bible, both goddesses stop the wheel.
