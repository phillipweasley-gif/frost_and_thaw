## 1. The chapter in one line

**Question:** *What is burning under the tree, and where did the Summer Marshal go?* **Answer (as played):** *Avarice's engine is burning Sephek's heart, which holds Auril's power, on the tree's sap. The Marshal has been her "coat" for thirty years.*

**Does the game say the question?** Not in this chapter. Kelyn says it at the end of Part 06 (6.25): "Find our Summer Marshal. And stop whatever is burning the roots." Chapter 7 opens on a route ("Two ways down…", 7.01). After that the question comes back only in pieces: Myr's "It has carved her old roots into a machine" (7A.07), and Hyphora's "Below me: fire. The cold man's heart. A woman who counts" (7A.12).

**Does it answer what the last chapter raised?** Yes, and it goes further. Chapter 6 ended on "Summer has Sephek's heart" (6.14 goal) and a missing Marshal, and Chapter 7 settles both. But it also answers **Chapter 8's** question ("Who is Summer?") at least six times before Chapter 8 can ask it:

- the attendant: "Summer is a coat. Summer has been wearing a man for thirty years." (7B.11)
- Selrae and Alberica: "I didn't know it was her." / "Avarice." (7B.12)
- the voice in the boy's mirror (7B.13)
- Mira's "Our little failures" (7B.16)
- the lab notes (7B.28)
- Var's echo: "She's wearing me, isn't she." (7B.32)

The player also watches Avarice step out of a mirror and take Var (7B.32).

## 7. Game fit

**Structure.** This isn't IGDA's parallel paths, where the player takes one of several branches (DeMarle p. 103). You play both roads, which makes it DeMarle's **gating** (pp. 101–102): any-order challenges under a linear checkpoint, the forge door. She gives two warnings:

- A gated story feels rigged when nothing in it responds to the order the player chose (p. 102).
- Paths that rejoin must each feel "significantly different" (p. 103). Berger allows dovetailing at milestones if the paths differ in "tone, characters status, and points of view" (pp. 98–99).

The difference is there. Road A ("through the people who tend it") is healing and naming. Road B ("through the city's own works") is complicity: Hew, Selrae, Mira and Darrus's crates. That's *tending against taking*, a real pair.

The fold is where it fails. Neither road knows about the other, so the second one repeats the first one's reveal, and the rejoin leaves the ambiguous state DeMarle warns about (p. 107): once Selrae is home, Kelyn knows "All of it", and in Ch8 she still greets Var as Var.

**Momentum and the sag (Seger pp. 78–98; Mills pp. 113–129).** Road A is a cause-and-effect chain, where each scene "contains the seed of the next" (Seger p. 79): "Help Yarro. Then ask." → "Go to the Mnemosyne" → "the water has gone bad" → "Something down there wouldn't let go."

Road B's chain breaks three times:

1. **A link only the goal box makes.** "Someone below has a key" (7B.05) is something no character has said.
2. **A detour between key and lock.** Selrae's key is in hand when a trainer's sight line pulls you back up to the boy and out to Mira's ship.
3. **A lock that adds length, not a decision.** Six maps down, the door grows a second lock.

The lower zones show Seger's Act Two symptoms point for point:

- **Obstacles that force no new decision** (pp. 82–83).
- **Talk in place of action points:** seven document reads.
- **The Rule of Three broken:** Avarice is named or shown six times, and the White Womb six times (pp. 83–84).

There's no Mills "wrench" (p. 124), and nothing the party does makes things worse (p. 108). Her best cure, a choice between two rights (p. 129), does appear twice: the pale sovereign and the attendant. Those are the best scenes down there.

**Reward or wall?** On Road A, story is a reward. Fights end in short sequels that name the next goal, which is Mills's scene/sequel rhythm (pp. 108–109). Road B puts walls before play: six boxes of lab notes before a fight, Var's 19-box vision before his battle, the Scriptory before an ambush. It also buries its sequels: 22 boxes after Hew, 22 item boxes after the Mycolith, and six silent boxes at the end (Berger pp. 111, 149).

**Does the conflict match the challenge?** On Road A, yes: creatures driven mad by heat, and puzzles that are acts of care. At the top of Road B, yes: you beat the sap-dazed awake ("I can hear my own name again"). In the lower zones, no. Generic constructs and lift ambushes stand in for a story about complicity and stolen time.

**Meaningful choice (Berger p. 94).** Mira's core, Selrae, the attendant and the pale sovereign pass. The barracks, Myr's oath and the fourth pod don't. Road order is nearly cosmetic. The cheapest fix in the chapter is DeMarle's acknowledgment of small actions (pp. 104–106): a few lines that name what the other road found.

## 8. Verdict

**What's working**

1. **Road A is a model.** Every scene hands off to the next. The puzzles are acts of care, and the boss voices the foundation pillar ("STILL. STAY. STILL.") so that Wyn can answer it. Its 6.9 should be the chapter's floor.
2. **The roads are a real thematic pair.** Road B's run of confessions (Hew, Selrae, Mira, the crates, Var) makes Rootspire complicit in its own fever. That sets up the Trials better than anything in Ch6.
3. **The mirror scenes earn rule 1.** The boy's smirking reflection, the twin mirrors and Meat Meat's burned hands ("Meat Meat had a mirror lady too. Meat Meat got away. You will.") are the part's best character beats, and they cost her something.
4. **Two choices between two rights** (the attendant, the pale sovereign), plus Mira's core, which reaches Ch12.

**Top 3 story problems**

**1. Chapter 7 gives away Chapter 8's turn.** Six vehicles answer "Who is Summer?", the player watches Avarice take Var, and her whole plan is read off walls.

- *Why it hurts:* SC 8.06's "The face cracks like a mirror" becomes a confirmation, not a reversal. Kelyn's "Var? thank the seasons" turns false. A twist needs a path the audience was invited down, not one they were told (Seger pp. 86–88). Avarice is also overexposed before her boss (Berger p. 88), and the Rule of Three is broken (Seger pp. 83–84).
- *Fix:* keep one ambiguous clue per road: Hyphora's "A woman who counts" and the attendant's "Summer is a coat", without "wearing a man for thirty years". Selrae says "I didn't know what *she* was", and Alberica's "Avarice." moves to SC 8.06. Move Var's vision to after the Ch8 fight, where "She's wearing me, isn't she" becomes grief, not exposition. Move the lab notes, the Scriptory and the pages to the Ch8 Barricade or to Trials evidence. Their flags (`knows_xardorok`, `corsair_arms_deal`) are read nowhere now.

**2. The roads never fold back into a scene, and there's no chapter climax.** The door opens in six silent boxes. Road order changes two lines, and the heat never rises. TP2's dark moment and its new stimulus are three maps apart.

- *Why it hurts:* a gated story that ignores the player's order shows its rigging (IGDA p. 102). Paths should rejoin at a milestone that they each colour (Berger pp. 98–99). The two halves of a turning point must come together, not leave the audience "in limbo" (Seger pp. 47–48).
- *Fix:*
  - Write a meeting scene that reads both roads' flags (sketch below).
  - Give the second road one opening line naming what the first found. *(Suggestion:)* Hew, if Road A came first: "The myconids sent word. Their god's afraid. So am I."
  - Make the clock real. After each badge, a heat-pulse beat plays in the city: the Prologue's shudder, which the bible calls "the forge's first heat-pulse", paid off. The road you leave for second gets one notch worse (Mills p. 192).

**3. Road B's lower zones are the Act Two sag, and the people who should own the chapter don't get scenes.** Eighteen scenes on six maps average 4.8: obstacles with no decision, a second lock, seven reads, and four losses that send you a long way back. Meanwhile Glimmer, who dies next chapter, has no scene, and Wyn never gets to ask Hyphora their question.

- *Why it hurts:* momentum comes from cause and effect, not more corridors (Seger pp. 79–80, 94). A middle needs a wrench and some doubt (Mills pp. 124, 129). A death lands only after the game has slowed down to build the bond (Berger pp. 103, 118).
- *Fix:* cut the lower zones to one scene sequence of three rooms (Seger pp. 90–93):
  1. The Mirror Garden, with the worst-self petals made mandatory.
  2. The Cocoon Gallery: the Rootwarden, the scout, the pale sovereign.
  3. The Mirror-Root Vault, with the Sigil.

  *(Suggestion:)* add one wrench. At the time-slip, Glimmer is caught behind a mirror-flower ("Seven years behind a mirror… don't stare"), and you go in after him. That gives him his scene, and it makes the lag rule cost something.
