# 01 · Chapter 1: Targos (story review)

Reviewed from the as-played script `docs/review/script/01.md` (New Journey), against the bible's Chapter 1 section, the one-page story, the five rules and the Heralds' sheets, and `act1_script.md` SC 1.01–1.08. Line, staging and bug findings are in `docs/review/notes/01.md` and are referred to by number, not repeated. The bible calls this chapter "The Convergence"; the script calls the part "Targos".

---

## 1. The chapter in one line

**As the player would state it:** "Who are these five strangers, why do their cold trinkets hum together, and what do they have to do with the woman in the furs?" The answer the chapter gives: the trinkets were left for them on purpose. "That's not luck. That's bait. ... For you. Somebody wanted you here." (Epheris, 1.04). The compass points south-east, to Dougan's Hole. The woman stays unexplained (named in Ch2).

**Does the game say the question?** Yes, which is to its credit. "ALBERICA: Bait for what?" (1.04) asks it aloud, and the goal states it: "Find who's calling us. South-east." That is the bible's whole Act I throughline, said in the chapter that sets it up, and it is paid off in Ch4 ("THREDRA: I sent them. My watchers watched you aboard.", 4.22).

**The bible's question, as played.** "Who are these people, and what are these relics?" Half answered. *What the relics are* is answered clearly. *Who these people are* is answered only at the level of names and one joke each. What each Herald wants (Alerion's dead brother, Reptin's broken balance, Shade's fear for Shimmer, Shimmer's escape from Auril) lives in table lines nobody can reach and in the optional rest scene 1.07 (notes S1, E19).

**Does it answer the question the prologue raised?** The prologue ended on "what's in the glass, where did it take me?" Chapter 1 answers *where* only through the map and the people, and never *when*. "ALERION: You fell out of the air. ...Nobody else saw. Keep it that way." is the only line in the chapter about what happened to you, and nobody follows it up, in this chapter or the next four.

**Overlap: Reptin and Shade.** Both do the talking. Shade negotiates ("We're in shipping"), sends the party to Epheris, and is billed as "smuggler and negotiator"; Reptin is "the party's face" and says "Leave the talking to me." In 1.03 they take turns at the same job. Seger: two characters with the same function weaken each other (p. 261–262). The bible already has the fix: Shade's real function is Shimmer's protector ("they can talk anyone into anything except staying"). Give Shade the protector beats on the golden path (watching Shimmer at the woman's look, sitting between Shimmer and the door) and leave the deals to Reptin.

**Missing: the Heralds' wants.** Seger's spine is motivation + goal + action, with motivation best shown in the present (p. 224–231). On the golden path the five are introduced by their *objects*. Their wants are written (the table lines, 1.07, the Heralds menu) but placed where most players won't see them. The result is that "Who are these people?" is the half of the chapter's question it doesn't answer.

The player is **active in play and a passenger in story**. You walk, you fight the gym, the five follow you; but nobody asks what you want, you never decide to go with them, and the one question put to you is forgotten as soon as it's answered. Seger's passive-protagonist warning (p. 232) and Berger's meaningful-choice test (p. 94: change the outcome, move the plot, or reveal something) both fail on the tavern choice: Alerion's "Clearly." reveals a little of Alerion, nothing of you, and nothing changes.

There's a better role sitting unused. The player is the one person in the room who has *heard the legend*: the mural's "one with a lute ... one with a wolf ... one half in shadow, with a knife" (0.11) and the bard's "the dwarf sang, and the wolf-man wept" (0.04). In 1.03 the player meets the dwarf with the lute and the man who'll become the wolf. That's dramatic irony waiting to be used (Seger's two-person POV: the audience knowing more builds tension, p. 101–102). A recognition line or two would make the player the witness who knows how the story ends, which is the bible's own arc for you ("from fan to peer"). (Caution: the bible doesn't say which carved figure is which, and the five in Targos aren't the five who come home, so only the lute and the wolf are safe to name.)

## 7. Game fit

- **Story as reward or wall?** The first visit to the tavern is a wall: 38 boxes before the first input, 47 in all (notes E15). After that the chapter is built the right way round: Epheris and Naerth are rewards for exploring, and the gym's story beats are rewards for winning. The misdirected signposting (notes E16) turns the reward into a hunt.
- **Scene/sequel (Mills p. 108).** The gym is the one full gameplay scene (goal: the seal; conflict: Naerth's people; disaster: none, because you win). Its sequel, Naerth's "You're walking toward something", is good, but the chapter's emotional sequel (fear, a decision to go anyway) is 1.07, which is optional. On the golden path, the chapter has no sequel at all: the seal is won and the goal says "The south gate is open."
- **Story conflict vs gameplay challenge.** Partly matched. The gym is Naerth's Zhentarim, so the battle fights the chapter's local power. The chapter's real conflict (someone is luring them; a goddess is watching) has no gameplay in Targos, which is fine for a mystery, but it means the climax answers a different question from the one the chapter asked.
- **Meaningful choice.** One choice, thrown away (§4). The rest are side-quest yes/no prompts. The single most consequential action in the chapter (resting at Three Flags, which unlocks 1.07 and pays in Ch6) isn't presented as a choice.

## 8. Verdict

### What's working

1. **The catalyst.** The woman in frost-furs is the best entrance in Act I: silent, specific (she tilts her head at you), and over in eight boxes. It gives the cosmic antagonist a human face in the first scene she's in (Seger p. 237–240).
2. **The throughline is stated and paid.** "That's bait ... Somebody wanted you here" asks the Act I question aloud, sets a direction, and is answered by Thredra three chapters later. Momentum by cause and effect (Seger p. 79–80).
3. **Voices.** Every Herald passes the cover-the-names test in their first line ("Smuggling." / "No I don't." / "Don't."). Epheris and Naerth are distinct in one line each.
4. **The townsfolk carry real setups** (laughter on the wind, the man in shirtsleeves, Kip's list): Berger's short repeated beats for a long act (p. 90).

### Top 3 story problems

**1. The player has no goal and no role.** *Why it hurts:* the player's inciting incident (the fall) is never acknowledged after "Keep it that way", the one question put to them is forgotten, and they never choose to go with the Heralds. For five chapters the player plays someone else's quest. Seger's passive protagonist (p. 232); Berger's defined goal (p. 81–82) and meaningful choice (p. 94). *Fix:* (a) give the player a goal that converges with the Heralds' (00.md §8: the glass came from the hag's mirrors, the Heralds' lures point at the hag); (b) make the tavern choice remembered: read `told_somewhere` and `shimmer_warm` once each in Ch1–2 (notes E25), and add a fourth answer for New Journey, "I'm going where you're going," which sets a flag the Heralds react to; (c) let the player recognise the Heralds from the mural, one line each, so their role is the witness who knows the legend (§4). *Cite:* Seger p. 232, p. 101–102; Berger p. 81–82, 94.

**2. "Who are these people?" is answered with objects, not wants.** *Why it hurts:* the chapter promises five characters and introduces five relics. Their wants are written and good (the table lines, 1.07, the Heralds menu) but sit where the golden path doesn't go, so in Ch3 Shimmer's death and in Ch6 Glimmer's return rest on character work most players never saw. Seger: motivation shown as a present action (p. 224–231); Mills: backstory in small pieces at the moments the plot needs them (p. 59). *Fix:* the table-talk structure in the rewrite sketch below (each Herald's want in their own two or three boxes, chosen by the player), and put 1.07 on the path: the goal after the seal sends you to rest at Three Flags before the gate (notes E19). *Cite:* Seger p. 224–231; Mills p. 59, 108–110.

**3. Nothing cold happens in Targos after the first scene, so the chapter has no dark moment and no action point before its climax.** *Why it hurts:* from 1.04 to 1.06 the chain is bureaucratic (you need a seal to leave), and every threat (Sephek, the lottery, the witch) is reported, not seen. Seger: momentum through action points, not talk (p. 79–81); a turning point is a dark moment plus a new stimulus (p. 47–48). *Fix (suggestion, new invention):* one action point on the path, between the seal and the gate. The simplest uses what's built: 1.07 ends with Shimmer at the window and, on the snow outside the tavern, a line of bare footprints that don't stay ("His footprints don't stay", which is how 2.08 describes Sephek). Shimmer: "He's here." Morning: they're gone. That gives Targos its dark moment, makes the rest scene matter, and sets up Sephek's arrival in Bryn Shander as a pursuit. *Cite:* Seger p. 47–48, 79–81.

### Rewrite sketch: 1.03, "Five cold things"

The weakest important scene (6/10): it introduces the whole party and is the player's one chance to be part of it. It builds on notes E15 and S1 (table talks), and adds the player's role. New lines and mechanics are marked as suggestions.

1. **Close of 1.02 (on screen: the five at their tables, Ma Yarbroul, you at the door).** "Drinks are on the house, ducks. Please leave tomorrow." / Alberica: "Free drinks. See? The night's turning around already." Then control returns. *The cutscene ends at about 12 boxes.*
