# 15 · Side: Rootspire's quests and townsfolk (Act II) — dialogue review

Scenes are from `docs/review/script/15.md` (15.01-15.40). Code paths are under `game/Data/Scripts/998_FrostThaw/`; `048_X` means `048_Rootspire_X_*.rb`. Most of this part was never played (the script marks it "from the code"). Page cites are Martell, *Dialogue Secrets*, unless marked Seger.

## 1. In one paragraph

As lines, this is some of the best writing in the game. The townsfolk of the old tiers, the secret-holders on the Root-Tier and the Heralds' one-liners are short, specific and funny, and many have real subtext: Jorik's "HA! ...Ha.", Shivven's "Ask me why. No. Don't.", Ysera's "Nothing is wrong. All is well.", Hellen's "Spring doesn't shout, loves. Spring just stops watering." What goes wrong is above the line. **(1) Almost no briefing is a fight.** Quest-givers stand still and confess. The Lorn Scribe, Kethan, Rheven, Ivran, Eirwen at the pillar and Kelyn at the orb hand over their information with no pressure in the room, often four to eight boxes in a row (pp. 69, 144). The two that *do* have a weapon in the room (Felka's shard talking while she talks; the amber brick screaming while the assessor takes notes) are the best scenes in the part, which shows the fix works. **(2) Alerion has become the part's narrator.** After nearly every NPC reveal he explains it ("She's warning us. Without warning anyone else.", "G.A. Gorran Ashal-Forge. The Sap-Smith.", "Avarice's mark. On something from Auril's own plane."). That breaks his sheet (he answers the literal question and never explains a joke) and is See & Say (pp. 27, 37). **(3) Shared words.** A pet name is in 35 of Alberica's 50 lines ("darling" alone in 21), and "darling" is also in the mouths of Reptin, Lumea, Maera and an Autumn courtier; the seers' warnings share "something below"; five people dream "every night"; two speakers share "Somebody's praying for cold", two share "Everything is real somewhere", two share "something wants them to get back up", and Alerion says "I asked it to sit" in two scenes (pp. 35, 80). **(4) Lines that don't follow** cluster in the riddle chain and the Library: people answering questions nobody asked, "she" with no referent, and Glimmer quoting Brightmane on something Brightmane never said. And six side scenes hand the player the Chapter 8 twist ("Summer is a woman. A tiefling." "For horns.").

### The ten seers: do they at least sound different?

**In how they talk, mostly yes. In what they say, no.** Each has a lens in their first box (Lirielle scent, Serelle burial rites, Ysera souls, Bryn roots, Brogar a crystal arm, Arvoss stones, Thim animals, Viessa candles, Gurk a hammer, Yorin rhyme), and Yorin, Viessa, Bryn and Ysera keep their sentence shape when they warn. But the warning itself drops the lens and falls into one shared phrasebook:

- "Something below": Lirielle "Something below is burning the seasons alive." (026:177), Ysera "Something below hoards what must return." (048_B:238) and "Something below doesn't want to be seen.", Brogar "Nobody forges below.", Thim "From below." Cover the names on those and Lirielle and Ysera can't be told apart.
- "Every night" dreams: Lirielle, Rheven, Eren, Shivven, Serelle ("I dreamed it again"). Lirielle *reads the air*, yet her warning is a dream.
- "Scream": Lirielle's "Sap screaming", Rheven's "The tree screams in my dreams", Wyn's "Sap that's been made to scream", the Perfume Prophecy's sap "screaming in its channels".
- Gurk and Brogar are both smiths whose metal senses the forge (a hammer that talks, an arm that hums): the same device twice.

Fix, within dialogue: keep each warning inside its lens and out of the shared words. Lirielle smells it ("LIRIELLE: Your coat smells of charred violets. Where under the tree have you been?" *(suggestion)*); Serelle counts it in burials ("SERELLE: No bodies from the Root-Tier in a month." / "SERELLE: They go down. They don't come up." *(suggestion)*); Brogar keeps the arm and Gurk swaps the sensing hammer for plain smith's talk. If the story pass merges the seers into three (story review: Lirielle, Ysera, Yorin), these three are already the most distinct.

## 7. Top 5

1. **Add a weapon to every quest-giver's briefing, starting with the Lorn Scribe, Kethan, Ivran and Eirwen at the pillar.** The fact stays the same; something live goes in the room: the traitor's own Hall for Kethan, a frozen crewman knocking under the dock for Ivran, a dawn that really is coming for the Canticle, the Scribe's pages frosting as he talks. Keep Kelyn in earshot of Darrus instead of moving her away. Felka's shard and the amber brick show how well it works (Martell pp. 21, 144-149; reference §5 "deliver quest briefings with pressure on them").
2. **Take the narrator's job away from Alerion.** Cut or replace his ten-odd glosses (§4) with literal, flat lines ("Don't dive.", "Hooks catch things.", "Then we didn't hear anything."), and keep his one devastating line per scene. Let the player read the subtext (See & Say, pp. 27, 37; voices.md).
3. **Keep the Chapter 8 twist hidden.** Make the eleven rows in §4b: no "tiefling", no horns, and no "Avarice" next to fire or summer. Keep three "no face" clues (Razzilith, Maera, Dathra) as the set-up (Seger pp. 83, 86-88).
4. **Space out the shared words.** Cut Alberica's pet word to one a scene and none under pressure; take "darling" from Reptin, Lumea and Maera; give the seers their own lens instead of "something below… every night… screaming"; break the paired lines (Fisher/Ivran, Jast/Lumea, Rheven/initiate, Alerion's "asked it to sit", Eira's "loved the cold", the creed-written-drunk joke) (pp. 35, 80; cover-the-names p. 33).
5. **Fix the twenty lines that don't follow (§3).** Above all, plant Brightmane's "you'll know the fire when you see it" in 15.12 so Glimmer's three callbacks have a source; stop the Scribe, Eirwen and Kelyn answering questions nobody asked; give "she" a name; and make Hellen, Kelyn and Meat Meat react to what just happened (no psychic characters, p. 18; the bounce, p. 17).
