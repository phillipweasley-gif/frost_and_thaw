## 1. In one paragraph

Line by line, the trial week is some of the best writing in the game. Rheven's "Lift, or talk. Preferably lift.", Wyn's "I kept the door under those spores for three years. They kept me." and the refugee's "My boy was light. They took him first." all land, and the Heralds pass the cover-the-names test more often than not. What goes wrong is the shape of the scenes. A trial week should be one long fight, but hardly anyone here opposes anyone. The witness scenes are Martell's Prompter Exposition with a button. An "ASK: Ask X: '…?'" box comes up, the player says yes, the witness gives a past-tense block of facts, and then promises, in nearly the same words each time, to "say it to the crystals" (Martell, pp. 15–16, 126–127). He warns that courtrooms are where this pattern lives (p. 16). The courtroom itself then plays none of that testimony. The Verdict reads out a 26-name list, the Herald you choose to speak for the defence says nothing, and there is no prosecutor at all. So the climax of a trial chapter has nobody on the other side (p. 17). The same thing happens in smaller scenes. Gym 8's debate is four player choices that nobody answers. Selrae confesses her fear because the accused asks her to (p. 11). Phildendrin finds her lost daughter, and three boxes later she's a quest-giver with a checklist (pp. 18–19: "what you need them to say"). On top of that come some game-wide habits: Alberica's "darlings" in three scenes running, Meat Meat's "not crying" joke in Alberica's mouth, and characters who explain a vision or a ledger we've just seen (p. 37). One thing makes the courtroom fix cheaper than it looks: the code already has an in-voice testimony line for every witness (`WITNESSES`, 036:106-149). The Verdict only prints their names.

### Lines that don't follow

1. **10.04, 10.37 (the omens).** "Reptin does what a Warden of Life and Death does, and Alberica's blood freezes." fires between pressing A on the foreman and his reply, while Reptin is doing nothing (048E:91). "Reptin decides a life, gently…" plays as you step out of the Wall, where he hasn't decided anything.
2. **10.07.** "PILGRIM: And the other one who listens for her. Two of us is one too many." "Her" points at nobody; a first-time player can't parse it (047d:495).
3. **10.12.** "There and then, they ask the Rootwardens to make them stewards." No Rootwardens are present (036:686).
4. **10.14.** "EIRWEN: Every page: Targos's sap money… going north to a summer that never came." Rootspire is south of Targos; the refugee in 10.10 says "It went south, past the Wall" (036:778).
5. **10.15.** "PHILDENDRIN: (turning on the Council)…" No Council is in the cells (036:863).
6. **10.15.** "REPTIN: (quietly, to the guard) Give them a minute." Two boxes earlier, "the guard is asleep on his stool" (036:864).
7. **10.15.** "PHILDENDRIN: The Council's offered something other than the roots." When, and how? She arrived moments ago through a crowd (036:886).
8. **10.16.** "MEAT MEAT: No more deals with mirrors." Nobody has offered a deal; the line is copied from the hag's mirror in Ch 8 (036:913, 034_Chapter8:249).
9. **10.18.** KELYN's "…another explanation" answers a witness, Eira, who never speaks. This is already in notes §3.
10. **10.21.** "IVO: I'll testify. If Wyn clears my name." Wyn has just said "I'll tell them you held. Alone." (036:1122-1125).
11. **10.24.** "ALBERICA: …Bring a hanky." answers Selrae's first box (the sneezing children) and skips the confession about blight right before it, so it reads as a joke at her fear (036:1431).
12. **10.25.** "SELRAE: …I was wrong about the blight." Nothing in the run's path shows her the difference; none of the evidence branches played (036:1513).
13. **10.25.** "LUMINARA: And you want to know about the summer." Nobody asked (036:1540).
14. **10.28.** "ALBERICA: She was always so particular about who walked on her floors." "Always" claims a knowledge of this room that Alberica can't have (p. 18) (047d:615).
15. **10.31.** "EIRWEN: I felt a ripple when a Rootwarden went missing… Could the leaf be with them?" The librarian knows the answer by a feeling and hands it over as a question (pp. 15, 18) (047b:508).
16. **10.42.** "Who speaks for the Heralds?" Then nobody does; Kelyn reads (036:1799).
