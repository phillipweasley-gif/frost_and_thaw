# Pokémon Frost & Thaw — Story & Script

Oct 29, 2026 · @Phil

## Where the story stands

The current build has strong scenes but no spine. Nobody says why the Heralds travel the Dale, six villains take turns without connecting, and the game never decides whether Act I is a memory or real time travel. This rewrite keeps nearly every scene and map that works. It adds one throughline: **who called the Heralds north, and why**. It also adds a few firm rules about mirrors, time and Auril's Hearts of Ice that everything else hangs on.

The audit read all 29 scripts and every event on the built maps. These are the problems you felt in playtesting, and what the rewrite does about each.

## The story in one page

**Two goddesses want the seasons to stop, and five strangers keep the wheel turning.** Auril would freeze the world so nothing beautiful can ever end. Avarice would burn it into an engine she controls. The Heralds learn from Shimmer that there is always a third way.

**Prologue.** In Rootspire, the city under the World Tree, it is Year 400 of the Verdant Reversal and the Festival of Returning Light. You grew up on stories of the Heralds of Dawn and Dusk. Professor Wynsnow gives you your first Pokémon. Your friend Tamsin buys you a lantern set with "real Reversal glass". The ground shudders, the glass cracks, and you fall into it. *(In Herald Mode you begin on the ship north instead, holding your relic.)*

**Act I: The Rime.** You come out on the docks of Targos, 400 years in the past, two years into Auril's Everlasting Rime. Five travellers arrive that same night: Alberica, Reptin, Alerion, Shade and Shimmer. Each was drawn north by dreams and by a cold relic that turned up from nowhere. In the tavern a woman in frost-furs looks at each of them, then at you, and smiles. Epheris the relic dealer tells them the relics were made to be found. They are lures, and all of them point toward Dougan's Hole.

The party crosses the Dale under lottery law, earning each Speaker's seal as they go. Sephek Kaltro, Auril's coatless Chosen, hunts Shimmer, because Shimmer was once Auril's vessel and escaped. On the road they find Razzy, a voiceless goblin who escaped a laboratory with wyrm-blood in his veins, and save him from a frost-light chasm as an egg. At the Bryn Shander lottery Alberica breaks a crowd's despair with her pendant, and Auril scars a rune into her chest. She is the next vessel. In Lonelywood, Sephek takes Shimmer. She refuses Auril a second time and gives her life "for them". Meat Meat, Razzy's friend from the same lab, arrives and speaks his name. The Wennen sing Shimmer's heart into a frost egg, and Shade carries it south. In Dougan's Hole a hag has frozen the townsfolk into her mirrors. She is Granny Thredra, the one who called them. She wants the Heralds for Auril's next vessel and the Chasm's hidden heart for herself. In the Cackling Chasm the party, and you, shatter her mirrors. Centuries of stolen time pour into the last root of an ancient World Tree. A golden acorn takes root. Razzy's egg and Shimmer's hatch together as twin dragons, and time comes loose.

**Act II: The Thaw.** You all fall out of the cracked lantern in Rootspire's market, an hour after you vanished. Inside the Auroral Wall 400 years have passed. Outside it, only weeks, and Ten-Towns is still frozen. The Heralds are legends here. Razzilith and Frostina, who waited 400 years for their riders, come down from the crown of the tree. At the Celestial Rift the sky turns to a mirror onto Brightmane's plain of stars. Shade steps through, and a minute later Glimmer steps back out: Shade, seven years older on the unicorn's side of the glass, a tabaxi now and her devotee, with the horn Shade gave up for Razzy given back and the cursed Rimefang still at his hip.

The city is stuck in an endless gold summer, and the Summer Marshal's seat sits empty. Two roads lead down. One goes through Hyphora's Undercanopy, where myconids tend the tree's newborn god and Wyn fights a fallen fungal god for its spark. The other goes through the Guild's mines and the Corsairs' mirrors to the Rootsun Forge, built in the hag's old lair. The two roads meet at the heart of the forge. There, the missing Summer Marshal turns out to be Avarice, the woman who made Meat Meat and Razzy. She has built an engine around Sephek's frozen heart and the tree's own sap to burn the seasons away, and a chardalyn dragon to carry her summer over the Dale.

Glimmer drives the Rimefang and his own dawn-fire into the engine. The dagger is reforged, the curse breaks, and Glimmer, who was Shade, is gone. At the Shrine the twin dragons bind Alerion and Meat Meat as Dragoons: Alerion to Frostina (winter), Meat Meat to Razzilith (spring). Avarice's borrowed body shatters and she flees into the mirrors. The dragon breaks out through the roots. You chase it on the twin dragons beyond the Wall, into the Dale the Heralds left only weeks ago, and bring it down over Bryn Shander. Refugees follow you home, among them Reptin's husband Anduin and Meat Meat's mother Phildendrin. The leaves fall. Rootspire arrests the Heralds for killing a councillor, and the Trials of the Roots begin. Each Herald answers for what they did, and the city votes to fill the two empty seats.

**Act III: The Frostmaiden.** With the engine dead, Sephek's heart is free. On the night of the vote the Fractured Halo raise him as the Frost Saint and steal Frostina. They mean to pour Auril into the vessel that once refused her, then take Alberica as well and rebuild the goddess in one body. The allies you voted for carry you across the Sea of Moving Ice to Solstice, the snowflake island. In the skull-crowned fortress of Grimskalle you face Auril's Four Tests: Cruelty, Endurance, Isolation and Preservation. Each Herald answers one their own way. Sephek, who wept for Shimmer, can be turned. Then Auril fights in her three forms.

At the end you choose. Shatter her, and the sun returns until her next winter. Let Alberica hold her still, and the wheel snaps back: time throws you to the start, again and again, until you choose differently. Or take Shimmer's third way: winter goes back onto the wheel as one season among four, and Frostina and Razzilith turn it. The Auroral Wall falls and the two times become one.

**Epilogue.** Rootspire has four seasons. Alberica, now Keeper of Time, sends an echo of herself back to guard the Chasm, which is the ghost you met there. Hellen's wall gets a sixth figure with your name under it. **Postgame:** Avarice's last shard waits in the White Vault, the buried city of Ythryn.

## Narrative pillars and tone

&#91;embedded content: narrative pillars · one rule, three pillars, one foundation\]

Every scene should serve at least one pillar. A scene that serves none is a cut candidate, which is how most of the current build's random text gets found.

- **Small warmth against a vast cold.** The Rime is huge and indifferent; what matters is the hearth someone keeps lit. Towns, gyms and side quests are about people holding on, so let jokes and food happen in the cold.
- **Found family across time.** The Heralds are out of their era and the player is out of theirs. Bonds formed in Act I pay off 400 years later, in Act II, through the people (and dragons) who waited.
- **Every mirror lies a little.** Mirrors, echoes and doubles always show something true and hide something else. Each reveal should cost a character something.
- **The foundation.** Auril wants to stop time and keep things forever; the heroes keep the memory and let the season change. When a scene has to choose, it chooses change.

**Tone.** Cosy, then cold. Light banter that stops dead when the wind picks up. Fairy-tale menace rather than gore. Sad moments get one quiet beat and no speeches.

## World bible

Five rules hold the setting together. Every scene must obey them, and every mystery in the story is one of them being uncovered.

### The five rules

1. **Mirrors hold frozen time.** A mirror that watches long enough keeps the moment it saw. Granny Thredra built her power by stealing moments: the people standing still on the Dougan's Hole ice are frozen inside her mirrors. Avarice learned mirror-craft from the hag's ruins. Reflections that lag half a beat, the Still Ones of Rootspire and the lantern that throws you back 400 years are all this rule at work. Breaking a mirror releases what it held.
2. **The Verdant Reversal was stolen time set loose.** Under the Cackling Chasm lies the last living root of the old World Tree that Auril felled when she made war on the seasons. When the party shatters Thredra's mirrors, centuries of stolen time pour into that root and into the golden acorn Reptin carries. A tree that needed 400 years grows in one night, inside a bubble of its own time.
3. **The Auroral Wall is the edge of that bubble.** Inside it, 400 years have passed. Outside, only weeks, and Ten-Towns is still in the Everlasting Rime. From inside, the outside looks frozen: snowflakes hang in the air and waves stop at their crests. When Avarice's engine dies, the Wall thins and the two times slide back together. That is why refugees who knew the Heralds a month ago can walk into Rootspire.
4. **Auril needs a vessel.** Casting the Rime every night leaves the Frostmaiden too weak to walk the world herself, so she pours pieces of herself into mortals. A failed vessel leaves a **Heart of Ice** behind. Shimmer was the first; she broke free, but her Lodestone of Frost stayed in her like an unlocked door. Sephek Kaltro is the current one, a drowned sailor whose frost-light heart is Auril's. Alberica is meant to be the next: the relics that called the Heralds north were lures. **Three hearts brought together could rebuild Auril a body.** The Wennen call that the Rewritten Auril.
5. **The wheel must turn.** Seasons, lives and grief all have to move. Auril wants stillness, so beauty never ends. Avarice wants an endless summer she controls. Both are a stopped wheel. The twin dragons were born to turn it, but they can't do it without riders. The Wennen call those riders Dragon Warriors: partners, not masters.

### Hyphora and the World Trees

Every world has a World Tree. Over thousands of years it bears thinking seeds, the Feycorn, and becomes a god. The old tree of this world was **Balanus**. Auril felled it and carved her Winter Stick from its frozen heart. **Erato** was one of its seeds; the golden acorn the priests of the Morninglord give Reptin is one of Erato's last. Planted in Balanus's last root by the Reversal, it grew into the **Heart of Spring**. **Hyphora** is that tree's young god: its soul, its voice in the roots, a name only the myconids know. It means "the thread that binds".

### Pokémon in this world

People and Pokémon live side by side, as in any Pokémon region. The monsters of *Rime of the Frostmaiden* are Pokémon or fusions: the yetis are Yetitan, the winter wolves are wolf fusions, and the mephits are Snorunt. **Twinning** (DNA Splicers) is the Zhentarim's trade in fused Pokémon, and it starts in Act I with Naerth. **Grafting** is Jade Cocoon-style merging. In Rootspire, Professor Wynsnow teaches it as a licensed art, and he gives you the licence as thanks once he has studied what happened to you. **Joining**, fusing a Pokémon into a person, is forbidden. Avarice did it to Meat Meat and Razzy.

## Characters

The Heralds drive the plot. Every character below wants something specific, and each Herald has one trait that is both their strength and their flaw. You, the player, never speak a feeling the player didn't choose: other characters react to your choices instead.

### You, and Tamsin

**You (New Journey).** A Rootspire kid who runs errands for Professor Wynsnow's College and grew up on Herald stories in Hellen's tavern. You are silent, and your choices are your voice. Your arc is from fan to peer: you find out the legends were people who chose, and that one of the figures carved on Hellen's wall, the one nobody could ever name, is you. Auril can't read you, because you aren't on her wheel.

**Wyn Mode.** Wyn takes your place. Wyn is a Rootwarden on festival watch, and the lantern pulls them back instead.

**Tamsin Brightrun.** Hellen's kid, about your age, determined to become a Rootwarden. Tamsin sees you fall into the lantern and spends the hour you're gone in a panic. Their starter beats yours, and their rivalry is a friend's jealousy: you got the adventure they wanted. They join the Rootwardens in Act II and stand by you at the Trials. Pronouns: they. Voice: fast, warm, competitive; teases you by your full name.

### The Heralds

**Alberica, Keeper of Memory.** Dwarf bard. Partner: Lutling (haunted lute line). She plays it badly and says so.

- **Wants:** to know why something keeps calling her north, and what her family's bargain cost.
- **Strength and flaw:** her memory. She holds on to everything, so she can't let anything go. That is exactly what Auril wants in a vessel.
- **Three words:** stubborn, wry, tender underneath.
- **Voice:** loud, warm and shameless; the heart of the party with the filthiest laugh in the Dale. Goes plain and quiet when it matters, then sings. Never says "I'm scared"; says "Something's pulling me north." (Full sheet in the Voices tab.)
- **Arc:** marked by Auril at the Bryn Shander lottery. In Act III Auril offers her the chance to keep every memory perfect forever, and she chooses a living memory instead: a song that changes each time it's sung. In the epilogue she becomes Keeper of Time and sends her own echo back to the Chasm.
- **Sample lines:** "I don't need it to make sense. I need it to stop." / "Write it down. No, sing it. Paper burns."

**Reptin, Warden of Life and Death.** Elf moon druid, exile, married to Anduin. Partner: Bulbasaur.

- **Wants:** to put things back in balance, because he once broke something trying to fix it.
- **Strength and flaw:** he gives too much. He feeds the starving wolves and takes the bite that makes him a werewolf.
- **Three words:** earnest, guilty, protective.
- **Voice:** the party's face: polished, polite and sassy, compliments used as weapons. Becomes blunt only when an innocent is in danger. (Full sheet in the Voices tab.)
- **Arc:** learns in the Chasm that his curse is a charge, not a punishment. In Rootspire, the Rootwardens treat him as a near-god, and Anduin fears what he's becoming. At the Trials he chooses to serve the tree rather than eclipse it.
- **Sample lines:** "Take me instead. If a life must be given, take mine." / "I'll keep it. The wolf. The watch. All of it."

**Alerion Froststride, Guardian of the Dark Half.** Eladrin Gloom Stalker. Purple eyes, a cold that never leaves his hands. Partner: Zorua (Rimirage line). Frostina's Dragoon (winter): from Ch9 her heart beats once in her and once in him.

- **Wants:** to undo the night a Winter Wyrm's curse froze him outside time while his tribe died, and his brother Talion with them.
- **Strength and flaw:** endurance. He can outlast anything, so he never asks for help.
- **Three words:** terse, watchful, quietly kind.
- **Voice:** aloof, few words, flat statements; blunt jokes that land a beat late. (Full sheet in the Voices tab.)
- **Arc:** in Rootspire he meets an echo of Talion his old necromancy left in the lake, and must let the echo go rather than keep it. On Solstice he takes the Test of Isolation and refuses to stay alone.
- **Sample lines:** "Single file." / "I was asleep." "No. You weren't."

**Meat Meat, Guardian of the Light Half.** Goblin Soulknife, Avarice's escaped Subject V-7. Long white hair, violet eyes, goggles pushed up. Partner: Noibat. Razzilith's Dragoon (spring). Says the bond feels like coffee.

**Dragoons** (from the Dragoon homebrew in the Alerion Guide). Dragoons are martial guardians bound to young dragons. They aren't riders at first: the bond begins as a whisper in the blood, and the dragon grows from a pocket-bound spirit into a companion and at last a battle mount. In the Dale they are "guides to young dragons, protectors of fragile seasons, and living vows that the sky will one day open again"; in the game that line is carved round the Shrine stump. Each bond has an essence (Alerion: Winter/cold; Meat Meat: Spring). What the game uses: the second heartbeat (Dragonheart Aegis: the dragon's heart beats once in its chest and once in the Dragoon's), Dragon's Leap and Rider's Dive ("gravity is a suggestion made by someone timid"; the Dragoon drops off the dragon onto the target and is caught), and Skybreaker Dive ("a falling star with a weapon at its heart"). The Chardalyn chase in Ch9 is the table's skill challenge: Razzilith and Frostina, adolescent, with Meat Meat and Alerion in the saddles.

- **Wants:** Razzy safe. Then, though she won't say it, a family.
- **Strength and flaw:** loyalty without limits. She will do anything for the people she loves, including things a court will call murder.
- **Three words:** nervous, fierce, funny.
- **Voice:** third person, simple words, exact feelings; playfully aggressive and devoted to coffee. "Meat Meat not crying. That is sap." (Full sheet in the Voices tab.)
- **Arc:** finds out in Rootspire what Avarice made her for. In the Forge she lets Autumn fall. At the Trials her mother Phildendrin, thought dead, speaks for her. On Solstice she takes the Test of Cruelty and refuses to be a weapon.
- **Sample lines:** "Find Razzy. Help Razzy. Razzy not dead. Just... very cold." / "Will Meat Meat stay a weapon, or be a seed?"

**Shimmer.** Rogue and smuggler, Shade's sister. Partner: Snorunt (Froslass line).

- **Wants:** to stay free of Auril, who wore her once.
- **Strength and flaw:** she trusts her gut, and her gut tells her to carry everything alone.
- **Voice:** quiet, quick, and dry. Says the true thing sideways.
- **Arc:** dies in Chapter 3 on her own terms, the third way. Reborn as Frostina, who doesn't remember being Shimmer but hums her song.
- **Sample lines:** "You feel that?" / "Not all fire dies. Some just learns how to freeze."

**Shade.** Smuggler and negotiator, Shimmer's sibling. They/them. Partner: Sneasel. Carries the cursed Rimefang dagger, and a unicorn's horn found in the snow the night they pulled Shimmer out of the white in Saltmarsh. Becomes Glimmer in Chapter 6.

- **Wants:** to keep their sister alive. When they fail, to keep what's left of her safe.
- **Strength and flaw:** they can talk anyone into anything except staying.
- **Voice:** smooth, wry deals; goes very quiet when it matters.
- **Arc:** loses Shimmer, finds her again in Frostina, gives up the horn to save Razzy, and answers Brightmane's call at the Rift: Shade steps through the mirror and comes back as Glimmer. Shade's story ends as Glimmer's does, at the Forge.
- **Sample lines:** "Fear has its uses." / "I'm not burying another one of us."

**Glimmer, Sparkbearer of Brightmane.** Shade, transformed: a tabaxi celestial warlock and Brightmane's devotee, carrying her unicorn horn. Silver-grey fur, amber eyes, midnight-blue coat, a sun-and-unicorn talisman (look provisional). Partner: Galarian Ponyta. He joins in Chapter 6, the moment Shade steps back out of the Rift.

- **Wants:** to finish what Shimmer started and carry her torch.
- **Strength and flaw:** hope that burns too hot. Auril warns that his grief burns brighter than love.
- **Voice:** warm, quick, slightly lost in time ("Unicorns are terrible at time").
- **Arc:** Shade, after seven years on Brightmane's plain of stars: a tabaxi, her devotee, carrying her unicorn horn and the Lumen Shard. Still carries the Rimefang, still cursed. In the Rootsun Forge he drives the dagger and his own dawn-fire into Avarice's engine, and the horn goes back to Brightmane with him. Rootspire remembers him as **the Dagger Reborn**.
- **Sample lines:** "That took longer than Brightmane promised." / "She said the torch was mine. I said that wasn't fair. She said that's what torches are like."

**Wyn, Warden of Shadow.** Circle of Spores druid and Rootwarden of 400 VR. They/them. Partner: Sableye (Sporeye and Myconarch line).

- **Wants:** to know why Hyphora called them away from their post at the Underdark door, the night the cultists broke through.
- **Strength and flaw:** they listen to the roots before people.
- **Voice:** soft, shy and warm; trails off and apologises, then says one brave thing that stops the room. Speaks of fungus the way others speak of family. (Full sheet in the Voices tab.)
- **Arc:** guides the Heartroot road, kills the Shadow Mycolith and takes its god-spark, and answers for leaving the door at the Trials.

### The twin dragons

### Villains

**Auril the Frostmaiden.** The goddess of winter's cruelty. She loves beauty, so she freezes it. She speaks rarely and never begs. In Act I she appears as the woman in frost-furs; in Act III she fights in three forms: the Cold Crone, the Brittle Maiden, and Winter's Womb. Her motive is grief turned into control. Line: "Your time in the warmth is ending."

**Sephek Kaltro.** A drowned sailor possessed by a frost druid's spirit, Auril's Chosen. He has no coat, leaves no footprints and carries ice for a weapon. He hunts lottery-cheaters and, in Act I, Shimmer. He weeps after killing her. Frozen in the Moon Temple, carried south by the Wennen, enshrined by the Halo for 400 years as "the Frost Saint", and raised again in Act III. He can be turned: "Her silence, not her return, honours her." Line: "The cold never touches me. You will not be so lucky."

**Granny Thredra.** A bheur hag, the laughing voice on the Dougan's Hole wind; Rootspire's stories call her Mother Thruuna. She sent mirror-watchers after each Herald and serves Auril, but she means to become the new Winter herself. Her voice is false hospitality: "Come in, dearies. Mind the cold." She is imprisoned in her own shards, and her echo haunts the Forge.

**Avarice.** A white-haired tiefling of the Arcane Brotherhood who "sees divinity as mathematics". She made Meat Meat and Razzy. In Rootspire she has worn the Summer Marshal's body for thirty years, building the Eternal Summer Engine and a chardalyn dragon. She calls the Heralds "my little failures". Escapes into the mirrors in Chapter 8; her last shard is the postgame.

**Matron Eline of the Still Sky.** Leader of the Fractured Halo, a serene fanatic: "Silence is mercy." She is Avarice's dupe in Act II and raises Sephek in Act III.

## Game-wide flowchart

&#91;embedded content: game flowchart · 3 acts, 2 forks, 3 endings\]

The golden path runs straight down the middle. The two forks rejoin: Roads A and B both have to be finished before Chapter 8 opens, and all three Chapter 12 crossings arrive at Grimskalle.

**Flags that steer it:**

- `fell_through` and `reversal` mark the two time jumps, and `loop_count` counts trips round the Ending C loop.
- `road_a_done` and `road_b_done` together unlock the Forge.
- `glimmer_joined` and `glimmer_gone` track Glimmer from the mirror to the Forge.
- `verdict_*` records the Trials' verdict, which Kelyn answers in SC 11.04. `council_<name>` records the Vote, which picks the Chapter 12 crossing and the Act III allies. If none of Mira, Rheven and Anduin wins, Lumin rings Angajuk's bell anyway: the whale route with two extra battles. Both read the `witnesses` counter.
- `test_*_broken` records each of the Four Tests.
- `ending_*` records the ending.

The hidden **Wheel** counter (see Systemic text) never blocks a route. It only adds the last Third Way scene at 4 or more (SC 14.01). A few flags are set but not yet read, such as `told_tamsin`, `norsu_calm` and `good_mead_saved`. They are hooks for companion chatter and epilogue variants, and nothing depends on them.

### Prologue: The Lantern (New Journey and Wyn Mode)

- **Maps:** 2050 Rootspire, 2063 College, 2053 Rooted Hearth (night 1 of the festival).
- **Beats:**
  1. Tamsin drags you to Wynsnow's College for your first Pokémon.

### Chapter 1: The Convergence (Targos)

### Balance notes

- Relics are held items worth a slot but not mandatory. Numbers are as built in `027_Relics.rb`.
- Blessings are passive and story-flavoured. None is stronger than a held item.
- Each personal quest is about 20–40 minutes, and each unlocks a witness. Finishing one adds 1 to the `witnesses` counter, which the Verdict (SC 10.08) and the Vote (SC 10.09) read. Canticle of Seasons also sets `canticle_learned`. In Act I, Kip's Cups sets `kip_list` (read in SC 2.05) and Moon Temple Sanctum sets `sanctum_dream` (read in SC 6.06).
- **The Verdict.** Three or more witnesses gives an acquittal. One or two gives guilty with a fine and service. None gives guilty with a geas, a cosmetic curse that lifts when the Council sends you north in Chapter 11.
- **The Vote.** Two seats. You can back two candidates; the number of witnesses decides how many of your two win.

## Systemic text

Signs, barks, companion chatter and item text are where the old build dumped lore. The rule now: **every line tells the player something they can use, or reacts to something they did.** Lore that does neither is cut. The cut list is at the end of this section.

### Signs and books

- One idea per sign, two message boxes at most, in a voice that belongs to someone in the world.
- Use the medium the world has: carved boards and painted shutters in Ten-Towns; light-written crystal pages and root-carvings in Rootspire.
- Long reads go only in safe places (inns, libraries), are always optional, and are never the only source of something the player needs.
- Example (Bryn Shander lottery board): "NEW MOON DRAW AT SUNDOWN. ALL NAMES. BY ORDER OF THE SPEAKER." Someone has scratched underneath: "all?"

### Companion chatter (the Heralds menu)

The existing `PARTY_CHAT` system stays. New rules:

- Each Herald has one line per area that **reacts to the current chapter flag**. Nobody talks about Razzy before SC 2.02, or about Glimmer before SC 1.07.
- Choices echo back. After `fed_pack`, Reptin's Ch5 line: "I keep hearing them. The wolves. They sound fed."
- Gone means gone: Shimmer has no lines after SC 3.04; Glimmer none after SC 8.07. Their spots in the menu show a short memorial line instead ("Shimmer: *Keep going.*").
- Herald Mode: your own Herald's lines become inner thoughts (the existing `as()` helper).

### The Wheel
